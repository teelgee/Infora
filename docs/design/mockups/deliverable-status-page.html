The deliverable status page, and the status bar on the review

Two screens. Clicking a reference on the review schedule lands on the status page — everything about the deliverable before opening the review: progress, people, metadata, documents, transmittals. "Open the review" leads to the review interface, which carries the same status bar at the top. The bar's four states — submitted, in review, responding, complete — are driven by stored state, never a timer.

Design decisions in this mockup
The four states, mapped to the model Submitted — the version is promoted to the library and the owner has acknowledged it into the round. In review — pen with the reviewers. Responding — pen with the authors. Complete — the lead reviewer's accepting verdict closed the cycle. A non-accepting verdict loops: round 3 restarts the bar at Submitted, and the prior round compresses to the one-line summary beneath.
The status page sits between the schedule and the review Clicking a reference on the schedule lands here, not in the round. Everything a coordinator needs before opening the review is on one screen: who is involved (with reviewer confirmation state), the deliverable's metadata and state, every version with its renditions, and the transmittal record. "Open the review" is the single primary action.
Open decision — who sees the distribution list The transmittal table shows the recommended default: the owner sees all recipient organisations and acknowledgement states; a receiving organisation sees only its own row; the producing organisation sees that issues occurred but not the competitor list. Follows ADR-0035's "the audit is the owner's record" — needs your yes/no before ticketing.
