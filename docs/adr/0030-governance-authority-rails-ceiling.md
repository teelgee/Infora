# ADR-0030 - Governance authority: the owner sets the project's rails as a ceiling, members inherit and may tighten-only on their own work, the effective rail is the most-restrictive intersection, and every rail change is audited

**Status:** Accepted (2026-07-01). Mirrors the access-model ceiling (ADR-0015 two-level access, ADR-0016 permission model, ADR-0017 authoring-side appointment, ADR-0018 ethical walls / private floor) applied to governance. Discharges the "governance authority" candidate ADR. Completes the deploy-time rails-inheritance reference left open in ADR-0028 §8 (custom-agent deployment) and formalises the authority model stated in principle in the governance component doc and the CONTEXT.md **Rail** term.

**Context:** Rails (register, negative constraints, confidence calibration — the generation-time governance rules) were agreed in principle to carry an authority model, but which party may set, tighten or loosen them, and how a member's tightening composes with the owner's, was never fixed. Governance and access face the identical problem — a subordinate scope must be able to narrow within a superior one but never pierce it — so governance should not invent a second authority shape; it should mirror the access ceiling exactly, so the two share one invariant and reason the same way.

**Decision:**

**1. The owner sets the project's rails — the ceiling.** The project owner sets the rails every agent and member on the project works within. This is the governance ceiling, the counterpart to the owner's control of the access model (ADR-0015). It is the floor of protection the owner guarantees for output produced on its project, because it is the owner's project record and, ultimately, exposure.

**2. Members inherit, and may tighten-only on their own work.** A member inherits the owner's rails and may make them **stricter** on its own private work — never looser, and never stepping outside them — exactly as a Participant grant narrows within Membership and never pierces an ethical wall (ADR-0018). A member cannot relax a rail the owner set; it can only add constraint to its own output.

**3. The effective rail is the most-restrictive intersection.** Where several rails apply to one generation — the owner's project ceiling plus any tightening a member has added to its own work (and, later, per-workflow or per-agent tightening) — the rail enforced at generation time is the **intersection**: every applicable constraint holds simultaneously, and the strictest wins on any axis. Composition is therefore monotonic — adding a rail can only ever restrict, never permit — which is what makes "a member can only tighten" true by construction rather than by policy.

**4. Rails are expressed at scopes, composed by that intersection.** A rail attaches at a scope: **project** (the owner's ceiling, always present), and — tightening only — **member-private work**, and later **per-workflow** and **per-agent**. The generation-time engine collects every rail whose scope covers the current generation and applies their intersection (§3). Finer expression (per-capability rails, richer constraint types) can be added within this model without changing the authority shape; the model, not the current catalogue of scopes, is what this ADR fixes.

**5. Deployed custom agents inherit the owner's rails at deploy time.** An org-built agent (ADR-0028 §8) deployed into a project is bound by that project owner's ceiling at deploy time. It may carry its own organisation's tightening on its own private work but can never loosen the owner's rails — the same tighten-only rule as any member, applied to the actor rather than the person. This is what makes the build-your-own-agents vision safe: a custom agent cannot import a weaker governance posture into a stricter project.

**6. Every rail set or change is audited — configurable, never silent.** Setting or changing any rail, at any scope, writes to the audit trail (component 3); the level of detail is configurable but the fact of the change is never suppressible. A tightening, a loosening-attempt (rejected), and the owner's ceiling changes are all recorded, so the governance posture in force for any past generation is reconstructable — the governance counterpart to the append-only agent-execution trail (ADR-0028 §3).

**Liability posture:** Infora ships rails so output *can* meet regulation; an organisation that erodes its own rails (within the tighten-only limit, it can only make them stricter, but it can also decline to add protection Infora offers) owns that liability, because it is the firm's name on the submission, not Infora's. The ceiling protects the owner's record; beyond it, responsibility sits with the producing firm.

**Consequences:** Governance and access now share one invariant — a subordinate scope narrows within a superior one and never pierces it — so the two can be reasoned about, and audited, the same way, and a reader who understands the access ceiling understands the rails ceiling. The engine gains a defined resolution step before generation: collect applicable rails by scope, intersect, enforce; this is a deterministic (doing) leaf in ADR-0027 terms. The data model gains rails as scoped records with an audited change history. Custom-agent deployment (ADR-0028 §8) has its governing rule and no longer forward-references a "forthcoming ADR." Honest limits, left open as tuning rather than authority questions: how grounding *thresholds* are calibrated and what exactly happens to withheld output; audit **retention and export format**; and deploy-time validation that a tightening is well-formed. These are governance-component detail (tracked in the grill doc), not changes to the authority model this ADR fixes.
