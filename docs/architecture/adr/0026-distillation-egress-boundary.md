# ADR-0026 - The distillation and egress boundary: a firm's portable org context is kept clean of a client's confidential facts by two mechanisms — the environment makes another client's material unreachable elsewhere, and a destination rule restricts what may feed the portable store to the firm's own material with client facts stripped; the moat compounds by retrieval and codified workflows, never by training on customer data; a deliberate human egress stays a tracked, accountable act; and Infora's own domain layer improves from de-identified cross-organisation craft that rests only in readable, versioned artefacts, never in weights

**Status:** Accepted. Builds on ADR-0025 (the assembly environment), ADR-0020 (org context is "a read source, never a destination" — this ADR supplies the mechanism that assertion lacked), ADR-0011 (accepted deliverables auto-admit to the library) and ADR-0021 (the informing channel is classified by ownership). Refines ADR-0018 (adds the inbound question to the private floor) and ADR-0016 (a share grant conveys read-for-project-use, not a licence to absorb into portable IP). Relates to ADR-0027 (thinking vs doing — codified workflows as a confidentiality-clean improvement path). **⚠ Amended 2026-08-26 (the signals session) — the cross-organisation prohibition recorded below is reversed.** Infora's own domain layer is now a permitted destination for de-identified behavioural craft, governed by a resting-place rule rather than by a partitioning rule; see the amendment block before the consequences. §24's training clause, Filter 1, Filter 2 and the environment mechanism are all unchanged — this amendment governs only what *Infora* may learn, never what one organisation may see or distil of another's.

**Context:** This is the hardest confidentiality question in the platform. Org context is portable — it travels to every project an organisation touches — while Knowledge is distilled from specific, confidential project work owned by a particular client. ADR-0020 asserted that org context is "a read source, never a destination" and that nothing distilled back into shared knowledge carries private context, but it never defined how that guarantee is produced. The threat the assertion must defeat is not the one the ethical wall handles. The wall isolates organisations from each other *on one project*; this threat runs on a different axis — a consultant C serving rival clients A and B carries one client's confidential positions, through its own portable store, into the rival's project. The wall never fires, because C is legitimately on both sides; the leak is C → C, project A to project B. ADR-0018's private floor protects org context from being *seen*; it says nothing about what may *enter* it. Separately, the access model left open what happens when private precedent text is baked into a deliverable that is then shared or promoted into the project library. And underneath all of it sits a question about how an AI platform "improves" at all: the way the moat compounds decides whether confidentiality can be enforced or is lost by construction.

**Decision:**

**Two mechanisms keep a firm's portable org context clean, not one "gate."** The distinction matters because they cover different cases.

*The environment makes another client's material unreachable elsewhere (structural, passive).* By ADR-0025, a request reaches only the channels of the environment it runs in. So when C works in B's project, A's grounded and guiding context simply is not present — there is nothing to filter, nothing to leak. This covers all ordinary work and needs no rule; it is a consequence of the environment model.

*A destination rule governs distillation (active, because the environment alone does not cover it).* When C works **inside A's own project**, A's documents *are* reachable — they must be, C needs them to do the job. So the one operation the environment does not contain is writing *into* C's portable org context from there: that could bridge A's reachable documents into a store that travels to B. The rule is therefore about where the output goes: **when the destination is portable org context, the only material that may feed it is the firm's own, and the client's facts are stripped.** This is tested twice, on ownership:

- **Filter 1 — ownership of the material.** Distillation into org context may draw only on what the firm *owns* — its own-authored deliverables and its own org sources — never on project content it can merely *see* (the client's library, owner-provided sources, other organisations' shared deliverables). The predicate is **ownership at distillation time, not authorship-ever**: having authored a deliverable that is then promoted to the owner's library does not make it distillable, because it is now the owner's property. *Visible ≠ distillable.*

- **Filter 2 — ownership of the fact within owned material.** A firm's own-authored deliverable still carries the *client's* facts — its costs, sites, positions, commitments. The firm typed them but does not own them. So even within own-authored material, distillation strips the project-identifying, client-confidential layer and keeps only the reusable expression, structure and method. The firm owns *how it argued*; the client owns *what it argued about*.

Admission is a **deliberate, abstracting curation act, never automatic.** An accepted deliverable flows into the *project library* automatically (ADR-0011: acceptance is the trust decision), but nothing flows into *portable org context* automatically. What is written there is a newly authored, abstracted object — a method note, a de-identified template, a clause skeleton — produced *from* the source, never the source carried over. A machine may *propose* the abstraction; a curator in the firm approves it and is accountable. The end-to-end engine is settled separately (Session 4); this ADR fixes that the path is manual and abstracts.

**The moat compounds three ways, and only the confidentiality-clean ones are used.**

1. **Retrieval** — an accumulating store of the firm's own, abstracted, attributable know-how (org context), governed entirely by the two mechanisms above. Every item keeps its owner and can be walled, scoped and deleted. This is the primary moat.
2. **Training** — baking knowledge into model weights. **Zero training on customer data** (the existing platform commitment, [overview](../infora-overview.md)); weights have no access control, cannot be walled or deleted, and can regurgitate, so a model trained on client work escapes every boundary here. A per-tenant fine-tune on the firm's *own, already-abstracted* material is a possible **future** evolution, explicitly not built now and flagged because once knowledge is in weights Filter 2 cannot be applied after the fact — any abstraction must happen *before* training, irreversibly.
3. **Codified workflows** — the firm's repeatable judgement compiled into deterministic code (ADR-0027). Confidentiality-clean and, unlike weights, **auditable**: a workflow can be read and confirmed to hold no client data. Subject to the same line as Filter 2 — codify the *method*, never a client's *constant* (a specific risk factor, a price). This is a firm's Custom workflow, held in org context.

So the moat compounds by **retrieval and codified workflows today**, both clean and attributable, with training deferred.

**The org-context-through-output question is resolved by the store being clean, not by a second mechanism.** If only de-identified know-how ever entered org context, anything baked into a deliverable *from* it is safe to share, and no redaction pass is needed at promotion (consistent with ADR-0020's no-redaction philosophy: the guarantee is produced once, at admission, and holds thereafter). Once precedent text is baked into a deliverable and accepted, it becomes the new project's owned fact, grounds as that project's fact, and is not traceable back to org context. The same firm-owned clause may appear in several clients' libraries — legitimate reuse of the firm's IP, not a leak; the client's *facts* never travelled, because the destination rule stripped them.

**The guarantee has an honest limit.** It defeats the *automated* leak (no agent path absorbs one client's material into a portable store) and the *accidental* leak (default promote/accept/share flows never carry it). It does not make a determined, authorised human forget what they read: a Participant can always copy material it can legitimately see out of a project into its workspace. That egress is a deliberate act; the platform's duty is to make it explicit and audited, leaving it accountable to the professional and contractual wall between the firm's engagements — not to pretend a technical wall prevents it.

**Confirmed 2026-07-29 (owner-ruled, the Ask agent-harness Phase 1 — [docs/build/plan.md — Slice Ask Wave 7](../../build/plan.md)) — the Teach capture path lands inside the boundary:** Ask introduces **Teach**, a deliberate, human-authored correction a user submits against a completed answer — a new capture path this ADR did not contemplate, and one that could plausibly have argued for a *third* destination: Infora's own shared domain layer, improving from one customer's de-identified, human-approved correction. **Ruled: no.** *"No aggregation across customers — Teach improves the individual org's signals."* So this ADR is applied unchanged rather than extended. A Teach submission never leaves the originating organisation; its **Steer** tier is exactly the curation act of the paragraph above, with Filter 1 (ownership of the material) and Filter 2 (client facts stripped, because org context is portable to the same firm's other clients) both intact, approved by **the firm's own curator** rather than by anyone at Infora. Because nothing crosses, the submission carries real identifiers rather than pseudonyms — pseudonymising would defeat the accountability this ADR requires of a curation act. Two consequences are accepted rather than left to be discovered: the eval harness carries two case libraries that never merge in either direction, and **Infora's own product quality does not improve automatically from customer corrections** — Infora's domain layer and regression suite grow only by Infora's own work, with an ordinary product-defect support channel sitting alongside Teach, since a bug report is not learning. The 2026-07-22 partitioning refinement below now governs Teach and Signals identically, which is the simplification the ruling bought. **Refined 2026-07-22 (Signal-corpus partitioning) — the training constraint extends to the behavioural-signal stream:** There is no contradiction between "zero training on customer data" and capturing accept/edit/reject Signals now: this ADR defers training and permits only a future per-tenant fine-tune on pre-abstracted, owned material; ADR-0028 §9 captures Signals now but defers the Signal *sink* (the moat-write loop) to Session 7; ADR-0002's guidance note uses Signals to *steer*, never to train or ground. The rule that keeps them consistent, made explicit here so Session 7 inherits it: **the Signal corpus is partitioned by organisation.** Any future training, fine-tune or signal-derived feature draws only on the originating organisation's own Signals and its own owned, abstracted material. **Pooling Signals across organisations to derive shared insight is prohibited.** This is *stronger than, and sits beside,* Filter 2's "own, abstracted material" rule: Filter 2 governs *what may be abstracted* into portable IP; this governs that the *behavioural-signal stream itself* never crosses the org boundary. The flywheel turns within an organisation's own boundary, never across it.

**Amended 2026-08-26 (owner-ruled, the signals session) — Infora's own domain layer becomes a permitted destination, and the governing question moves from *who may aggregate* to *where learning may rest*.** This **reverses** the 2026-07-29 Teach ruling recorded above and supersedes two sentences of the 2026-07-22 partitioning refinement — *"Pooling Signals across organisations to derive shared insight is prohibited"* and *"The flywheel turns within an organisation's own boundary, never across it."* **Why the reversal:** that ruling accepted in terms that *"Infora's own product quality does not improve automatically from customer corrections."* For a vertical product whose entire claim is that it knows the trade, that is not a confidentiality cost — it is a capability ceiling, and it constrains the wedge rather than protecting the wall. The cost was named honestly at the time; it is now judged too expensive to keep. The two flywheels are separated by this amendment and were previously conflated: **Infora's** improves the shipped product for everyone, and is what this block governs; **a firm's own** improves its portable layer alone, and is governed by the filters above, entirely unchanged.

**The rule that replaces it: the resting place, not the technique.** Learning Infora derives from customer behaviour may land only in artefacts a person can read, version, argue with and delete — Lens definitions, checker rules, workflow definitions and eval cases. It may **never** land in model weights: §24 stands unamended and its reasoning is why, since weights have no access control, cannot be walled or deleted, and Filter 2 cannot be applied to them after the fact. "Training" was always the wrong axis for this rule — a technique word cannot classify the cases that matter (a curated retrieval store, a few-shot exemplar, a generated prompt all sit between the poles), whereas the resting place classifies all of them. Every artefact this path produces is **authored by Infora**, ships in a versioned release to every customer including the one whose behaviour evidenced it, and is removed by deleting a line.

**Four conditions on the crossing.** (1) **Abstraction happens inside the customer's boundary.** What leaves is a characterisation of the change a human made — *"draft output asserting a significance verdict inline was edited to defer the verdict and cite the criteria"* — never the prose on either side of it. The `signals` row itself never moves. (2) **De-identification precedes egress.** This also settles a premise the reversal breaks: the 2026-07-29 ruling kept real identifiers on a Teach submission *"because nothing crosses"*, and now something does. (3) **Derivation collates across multiple organisations.** A convention observed at one firm is that firm's house style and stays in its own layer; a convention observed independently across several is the discipline's, and shipping it teaches the tool the trade rather than redistributing anyone's IP. This is simultaneously the design and its safeguard, which is why it is a condition rather than a separate protection clause. (4) **A named person at Infora is accountable** for each shipped artefact — the same shape this ADR already requires of a firm's own curator, moved one level up.

**What is not touched, stated so nobody reads this as a crack in the wall.** Filter 1, Filter 2, the environment mechanism and the destination rule governing a firm's **portable org context** are unchanged in every respect. This amendment alters no clause of what one organisation may see, distil or carry of another's; the axis it moves on is Infora↔everyone, not firm↔firm. The blast radius is the evidence: every citation of *"zero training on customer data"* elsewhere in the doc set (ADR-0037, ADR-0024, component 3's security posture) remains accurate without edit.

**Consequences, named rather than discovered.** The eval harness's two case libraries may now merge in **one** direction — customer-derived craft into Infora's regression suite, never Infora's cases into an organisation's library. Session 7 gains a second half: it inherited the within-organisation Signal sink and now also owns the craft path's shape and its abstraction step. The derived record is **new personal-data handling** — `signals` carries `resolved_by_participant_id` and free-text `edited_content` — so it lands in [personal-data.md](../personal-data.md) with its egress and deletion points named. **A deletion asymmetry is accepted:** ADR-0050 §6 lets a member organisation require complete deletion of its own Signals, but a craft observation already corroborated across firms and written into a shipped artefact does not come back — the artefact is Infora's authored work, de-identified and multi-org, not the customer's material. The DPA must describe this processing, which binds it to **`Q-035`**: the controller/processor model and this amendment are one conversation. **`Q-045`** is raised beside it — ADR-0050 claims *"no human holds a standing credential to customer data"*, and validating that the abstraction step works may require an Infora-side read over raw signal material that ADR-0050 did not contemplate. And until a second customer exists there is nothing to collate across, so the path is built and capturing while Infora's domain layer continues to grow by its own work, exactly as before. **A future prohibition is anticipated but deliberately not written now:** when firms configure their own layer (Slice E), that configuration is their IP and must not pool across customers even though behavioural craft may — a different rule from the one reversed here, to be written against the thing it governs when that thing exists.

**Consequences:** ADR-0020's "read source, never a destination" now has a mechanism, split honestly into the structural part (the environment, ADR-0025) and the one real rule (the destination constraint on distillation). ADR-0018's private floor gains an inbound rule to sit beside its outbound one. ADR-0016 gains *visible ≠ distillable*. ADR-0011's auto-admission is confirmed library-only. The moat's improvement engine is fixed as retrieval plus codified workflows, both confidentiality-clean and attributable, with training-on-customer-data ruled out now and any future per-tenant fine-tune constrained to pre-abstracted, owned material. The residual seam, named: Filter 2's abstraction rests on a human's judgement, so the worst remaining case is an under-abstracted artifact approved by a careless curator — reduced from an automated leak to a single accountable, auditable act. The distillation engine, provenance and currency are handed to Session 4; the codified-workflow constraint to ADR-0027; the Signal sink and any training-data handling to Session 7. Builds on ADR-0011, ADR-0016, ADR-0018, ADR-0020, ADR-0021 and ADR-0025.
