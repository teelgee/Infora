# ADR-0026 - The distillation and egress boundary: a firm's portable org context is kept clean of a client's confidential facts by two mechanisms — the environment makes another client's material unreachable elsewhere, and a destination rule restricts what may feed the portable store to the firm's own material with client facts stripped; the moat compounds by retrieval and codified workflows, never by training on customer data; and a deliberate human egress stays a tracked, accountable act

**Status:** Accepted. Builds on ADR-0025 (the assembly environment), ADR-0020 (org context is "a read source, never a destination" — this ADR supplies the mechanism that assertion lacked), ADR-0011 (accepted deliverables auto-admit to the library) and ADR-0021 (the informing channel is classified by ownership). Refines ADR-0018 (adds the inbound question to the private floor) and ADR-0016 (a share grant conveys read-for-project-use, not a licence to absorb into portable IP). Relates to ADR-0027 (thinking vs doing — codified workflows as a confidentiality-clean improvement path).

**Context:** This is the hardest confidentiality question in the platform. Org context is portable — it travels to every project an organisation touches — while Knowledge is distilled from specific, confidential project work owned by a particular client. ADR-0020 asserted that org context is "a read source, never a destination" and that nothing distilled back into shared knowledge carries private context, but it never defined how that guarantee is produced. The threat the assertion must defeat is not the one the ethical wall handles. The wall isolates organisations from each other *on one project*; this threat runs on a different axis — a consultant C serving rival clients A and B carries one client's confidential positions, through its own portable store, into the rival's project. The wall never fires, because C is legitimately on both sides; the leak is C → C, project A to project B. ADR-0018's private floor protects org context from being *seen*; it says nothing about what may *enter* it. Separately, the access model left open what happens when private precedent text is baked into a deliverable that is then shared or promoted into the project library. And underneath all of it sits a question about how an AI platform "improves" at all: the way the moat compounds decides whether confidentiality can be enforced or is lost by construction.

**Decision:**

**Two mechanisms keep a firm's portable org context clean, not one "gate."** The distinction matters because they cover different cases.

*The environment makes another client's material unreachable elsewhere (structural, passive).* By ADR-0025, a request reaches only the channels of the environment it runs in. So when C works in B's project, A's grounded and guiding context simply is not present — there is nothing to filter, nothing to leak. This covers all ordinary work and needs no rule; it is a consequence of the environment model.

*A destination rule governs distillation (active, because the environment alone does not cover it).* When C works **inside A's own project**, A's documents *are* reachable — they must be, C needs them to do the job. So the one operation the environment does not contain is writing *into* C's portable org context from there: that could bridge A's reachable documents into a store that travels to B. The rule is therefore about where the output goes: **when the destination is portable org context, the only material that may feed it is the firm's own, and the client's facts are stripped.** This is tested twice, on ownership:

- **Filter 1 — ownership of the material.** Distillation into org context may draw only on what the firm *owns* — its own-authored deliverables and its own org sources — never on project content it can merely *see* (the client's library, owner-provided sources, other organisations' shared deliverables). The predicate is **ownership at distillation time, not authorship-ever**: having authored a deliverable that is then promoted to the owner's library does not make it distillable, because it is now the owner's property. *Visible ≠ distillable.*

- **Filter 2 — ownership of the fact within owned material.** A firm's own-authored deliverable still carries the *client's* facts — its costs, sites, positions, commitments. The firm typed them but does not own them. So even within own-authored material, distillation strips the project-identifying, client-confidential layer and keeps only the reusable expression, structure and method. The firm owns *how it argued*; the client owns *what it argued about*.

Admission is a **deliberate, abstracting curation act, never automatic.** An accepted deliverable flows into the *project library* automatically (ADR-0011: acceptance is the trust decision), but nothing flows into *portable org context* automatically. What is written there is a newly authored, abstracted object — a method note, a de-identified template, a clause skeleton — produced *from* the source, never the source carried over. A machine may *propose* the abstraction; a curator in the firm approves it and is accountable. The end-to-end engine is settled separately (Session 4); this ADR fixes that the path is manual and abstracts.

**The moat compounds three ways, and only the confidentiality-clean ones are used.**

1. **Retrieval** — an accumulating store of the firm's own, abstracted, attributable know-how (org context), governed entirely by the two mechanisms above. Every item keeps its owner and can be walled, scoped and deleted. This is the primary moat.
2. **Training** — baking knowledge into model weights. **Zero training on customer data** (the existing platform commitment, [overview](../architecture/infora-overview.md)); weights have no access control, cannot be walled or deleted, and can regurgitate, so a model trained on client work escapes every boundary here. A per-tenant fine-tune on the firm's *own, already-abstracted* material is a possible **future** evolution, explicitly not built now and flagged because once knowledge is in weights Filter 2 cannot be applied after the fact — any abstraction must happen *before* training, irreversibly.
3. **Codified workflows** — the firm's repeatable judgement compiled into deterministic code (ADR-0027). Confidentiality-clean and, unlike weights, **auditable**: a workflow can be read and confirmed to hold no client data. Subject to the same line as Filter 2 — codify the *method*, never a client's *constant* (a specific risk factor, a price). This is a firm's Custom workflow, held in org context.

So the moat compounds by **retrieval and codified workflows today**, both clean and attributable, with training deferred.

**The org-context-through-output question is resolved by the store being clean, not by a second mechanism.** If only de-identified know-how ever entered org context, anything baked into a deliverable *from* it is safe to share, and no redaction pass is needed at promotion (consistent with ADR-0020's no-redaction philosophy: the guarantee is produced once, at admission, and holds thereafter). Once precedent text is baked into a deliverable and accepted, it becomes the new project's owned fact, grounds as that project's fact, and is not traceable back to org context. The same firm-owned clause may appear in several clients' libraries — legitimate reuse of the firm's IP, not a leak; the client's *facts* never travelled, because the destination rule stripped them.

**The guarantee has an honest limit.** It defeats the *automated* leak (no agent path absorbs one client's material into a portable store) and the *accidental* leak (default promote/accept/share flows never carry it). It does not make a determined, authorised human forget what they read: a Participant can always copy material it can legitimately see out of a project into its workspace. That egress is a deliberate act; the platform's duty is to make it explicit and audited, leaving it accountable to the professional and contractual wall between the firm's engagements — not to pretend a technical wall prevents it.

**Refined 2026-07-22 (Signal-corpus partitioning) — the training constraint extends to the behavioural-signal stream:** There is no contradiction between "zero training on customer data" and capturing accept/edit/reject Signals now: this ADR defers training and permits only a future per-tenant fine-tune on pre-abstracted, owned material; ADR-0028 §9 captures Signals now but defers the Signal *sink* (the moat-write loop) to Session 7; ADR-0002's guidance note uses Signals to *steer*, never to train or ground. The rule that keeps them consistent, made explicit here so Session 7 inherits it: **the Signal corpus is partitioned by organisation.** Any future training, fine-tune or signal-derived feature draws only on the originating organisation's own Signals and its own owned, abstracted material. **Pooling Signals across organisations to derive shared insight is prohibited.** This is *stronger than, and sits beside,* Filter 2's "own, abstracted material" rule: Filter 2 governs *what may be abstracted* into portable IP; this governs that the *behavioural-signal stream itself* never crosses the org boundary. The flywheel turns within an organisation's own boundary, never across it.

**Consequences:** ADR-0020's "read source, never a destination" now has a mechanism, split honestly into the structural part (the environment, ADR-0025) and the one real rule (the destination constraint on distillation). ADR-0018's private floor gains an inbound rule to sit beside its outbound one. ADR-0016 gains *visible ≠ distillable*. ADR-0011's auto-admission is confirmed library-only. The moat's improvement engine is fixed as retrieval plus codified workflows, both confidentiality-clean and attributable, with training-on-customer-data ruled out now and any future per-tenant fine-tune constrained to pre-abstracted, owned material. The residual seam, named: Filter 2's abstraction rests on a human's judgement, so the worst remaining case is an under-abstracted artifact approved by a careless curator — reduced from an automated leak to a single accountable, auditable act. The distillation engine, provenance and currency are handed to Session 4; the codified-workflow constraint to ADR-0027; the Signal sink and any training-data handling to Session 7. Builds on ADR-0011, ADR-0016, ADR-0018, ADR-0020, ADR-0021 and ADR-0025.
