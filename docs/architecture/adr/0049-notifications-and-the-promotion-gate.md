# ADR-0049 - Notifications are a persisted, event-shaped **record** whose visibility follows the thing it points at, with email as a nudge that carries a **pointer and a bare fact but never content** and resolves access at *send* time, not enqueue; the pilot set is **round-issued and @-mention** only; and promotion is **blocked** by open comment threads or open findings, overridable only on `Participant.elevated` and audited

**Status:** Accepted (owner-ruled 2026-08-01, design session 3). The **pilot event set** and the **promotion gate** are the owner's decisions — the gate ruled against Claude's recommendation, recorded as such below. The mechanics — the record shape, the visibility rule, the email-content rule, send-time resolution, and the elevated override — are Claude's, defended here. Refines **ADR-0032** (the review cycle, promotion) and **ADR-0048** (findings), which explicitly deferred the open-findings half of the promotion question to this session so both halves would be answered consistently. References **ADR-0018** (the wall), **ADR-0024** (revoke in the same transaction), **ADR-0047 §16** (Amazon SES as the transport), and the 2026-07-31 elevated-tier ruling.

**Context:** Nothing in the build tells anybody anything. T-112 stores `@`-mentions on `sandbox_comments` and renders them, and **no notification exists anywhere in the platform** — so a mentioned colleague learns they were mentioned only by looking. The failure this creates is not cosmetic and was named plainly when the question was raised: **a review cycle where nobody is told a round was issued runs on WhatsApp instead**, which means the coordination the product exists to hold moves to a channel Infora cannot see, audit or enforce a wall across.

The argument for designing this once rather than per-feature is that mentions, round-issued and lock-flipped all want the same channel. The counter-argument is that the pilot may need only one of them, and a general mechanism built against a sample of one is a guess. The session resolved this by separating two things the question had bundled: the **notification record** — persisted, in-app, read/unread — from the **delivery channel**, which is email. The record is cheap and is what makes "design it once" true; the expensive and risky decision is which events earn a delivery, because an event that fires often is the one that teaches a pilot user to mute the feature in week one.

The transport was fixed by ADR-0047 §16 (Amazon SES in `eu-west-2`), which is why this session could not have run before the infra session.

**Decision:**

**1. A notification is a persisted record, not a transient signal.** An event-shaped row: recipient, event type, a reference to the subject (the round, the document, the comment), a creation time, and a read state. It is the primary artefact; email is a nudge toward it. This is what allows a user who was away to catch up, and it is what makes the in-app unread list — not the inbox — the surface of record.

**2. The pilot set is `round-issued` and `@-mention`. Lock-flipped is deferred.** Round-issued is the named failure. `@`-mention is nearly free, because T-112 already stores and renders the mentions and only the *telling* is absent. Lock-flipped is deferred deliberately: it fires far more often than the other two and matters to one person who is usually already looking at the document, making it the event most likely to turn notifications into noise.

**The guard against designing a general channel from a sample of two is explicit: adding the third event must not require a schema change.** If lock-flipped cannot join the record as a new event type and a new subject reference alone, the abstraction was wrong and should be reworked then rather than defended.

**3. A notification's visibility follows the thing it points at — always, and never independently.** Access is resolved through the same path as the subject: a notification about a round is visible exactly to those who may see that round. A notification is therefore never a channel by which a fact reaches someone who could not otherwise reach it. This is the ethical wall (ADR-0018) applied to a surface that does not look like a data surface, which is precisely why it needs stating — the leak here would arrive through a feature nobody thinks of as crossing the wall.

**4. Email carries a pointer and a bare fact. It never carries content.** This is the load-bearing mechanic of the ADR.

An email leaves the platform. Once delivered it sits in a mailbox where Infora's access resolution no longer applies, where it survives revocation, and where it may be forwarded outside the organisation entirely. So the body is:

> *You were mentioned in a comment on Chapter 7 — Noise and Vibration.* [open in Infora]

and never the comment's text, never a finding's text, and — explicitly — **never a quoted rulebook clause**, which ADR-0048 §6 already made org-private. The subject line is bound by the same rule. Everything of substance requires following the link back into the platform, where the wall is real.

**5. Access is resolved at *send* time, not at enqueue time.** A notification queued for a user whose access is revoked before delivery **is not sent**. ADR-0024 makes a revoke effective in the same transaction as the read it governs; a queue that resolved entitlement when the row was written would quietly reintroduce the gap the revoke closed, and would do it in the one medium that cannot be recalled.

**6. In-app is the surface of record; email is the nudge.** Deliberately not built for the pilot: per-event-type preferences, digests, batching, and any notification setting beyond an on/off. At ≤ 25 users and two event types, preference machinery is configuration nobody has asked for.

**6a. Placement: two surfaces, deliberately — the rail *and* the project home.** *(Ruled 2026-08-01, refining §6 after the placements were drawn against the real shell; the original decision said only "an unread indicator and a simple list" and left placement to whoever picked up the ticket, which was too little to build from.)*

- **The rail carries the durable count.** A `Notifications` item alongside Home and Review, with an unread count. It is the only placement that survives **wherever the user is**, which matters because the person a round-issued event most needs to reach is the one who has been in the editor all afternoon and will not visit a home page.
- **The project home carries the actionable summary.** A **"Needs you"** panel directly above the work items and reviews, so a notification sits beside the thing it is about — *"Round 1 issued on Chapter 7"* immediately above Chapter 7's row is a stronger prompt than any badge, because the next action is already on screen. **The panel is absent at zero rather than showing an empty state**; that asymmetry is the design, not an omission.

Neither is sufficient alone: the rail reaches you but does not tell you what to do, and the panel tells you what to do but only if you go and look. **If only one can ship, ship the rail** — it is the one that addresses the failure `Q-012` was raised for.

**6b. The two surfaces are scoped differently, and that is intentional.** The **rail count is the user's own, across every project they can reach**, with the list grouped by project; the **project-home panel is scoped to that project**. The rail answers *"anything for me?"* and the home panel answers *"anything here?"*. Without this split a mention on a second project would be silently invisible, because the rail sits beneath a project switcher and would otherwise inherit its scope. At the pilot's one-or-two projects the difference barely shows; at ten it is the whole design, which is why it is fixed now rather than discovered later.

**6c. Rejected as the home for the record: a floating panel over the canvas.** It reaches the user anywhere — the same benefit as the rail, without spending a nav slot — but it **covers the work**, landing on the drafting canvas where a consultant spends the day, and it reads as **transient**, which contradicts §1: a notification is a durable row with a read state, not a toast that vanishes and takes the information with it. A floating, dismissible confirmation *is* the right shape for **something the user just did** ("Round issued to 3 reviewers") — that is a different mechanism and must not share this surface.

**7. Promotion is *blocked* by open comment threads or open findings.** Owner-ruled, and **against the recommendation on the table**, which was to warn and record the warning. The recommendation's argument, recorded so a future reader knows it was weighed: blocking makes Infora the arbiter of professional judgement, which sits awkwardly beside the liability model in which the consultant carries the judgement and Infora assists (the 2026-07-12 ruling); and a hard gate can teach people to close threads without resolving them, which destroys the signal the gate was protecting.

The owner's ruling is that nothing reaches the controlled library with loose ends. **Threads and findings are treated identically**, which discharges ADR-0048's deferral: the surface never has to explain why one list stops you and the other does not.

**8. The block is overridable only on `Participant.elevated`, and every override is audited.** This is Claude's mechanic, added because a block with no escape is not a stricter version of a gate — it is a deadlock. A finding raised against a paragraph that a later redraft deleted, or a comment thread whose author has left the project, would otherwise prevent promotion permanently with no lawful action available.

The override reuses the tier ruled on 2026-07-31 rather than inventing a bypass: `Participant.elevated` already holds close-as-rejected and owner supersede/withdraw — the actions heavier than a project role should routinely carry — and forcing a promotion past open items belongs in exactly that company. **An ordinary Participant cannot pass the gate**, so the block is real; an elevated one can, and the record says who did it and what was open at the time.

**Consequences:** The platform gains its first notification primitive and its first outbound email path, which is a new egress surface and is why decision 4 is written as a hard rule rather than a guideline. `Q-012` closes, and with it the last thing standing between the review cycle and being usable in practice. ADR-0048's deferred promotion question is answered in the same ruling as the comment-thread question, as it asked to be. Promotion acquires a precondition it did not have, which means the promotion path must be able to *count* open threads and open findings — a query the findings schema (T-328) must support and which is a real dependency, not a display concern. The rail gains a nav item and the project home gains a panel, both inside T-331's existing scope — two small additions rather than one. The elevated tier gains a third power, and the 2026-07-31 ruling's deliberate narrowness is preserved: this is a capability on a Participant, not a new standing role, and ADR-0016 does not reopen.

**Considered and not taken.** *Warn and record rather than block* — Claude's recommendation, overruled by the owner (decision 7). *Blocking on threads but only warning on findings* — rejected as splitting one rule into two the surface would have to justify. *An absolute block with no override* — rejected in decision 8 as a deadlock rather than a stricter gate; flagged to the owner as a departure from a literal reading of the ruling rather than folded in silently. *Delivering content in email* so a user can act from the inbox — rejected in decision 4; convenience bought with a permanent, unrecallable copy outside the wall. *Per-event notification preferences and digests* — deferred in decision 6 as configuration nobody has asked for at pilot scale. *A floating panel over the canvas as the home for the record* — rejected in decision 6c; it covers the work and reads as transient, though the same shape is right for confirming an action the user just took. *A single placement rather than two* — considered and rejected in 6a: the rail reaches you without telling you what to do, and the panel tells you without reaching you. *Including lock-flipped in the pilot set* — deferred in decision 2 as the event most likely to make the feature feel like noise.

**Amended 2026-08-27 (the document-status session) — a third pilot event, joining under decision 2's guard exactly as the "Open" note below anticipated.** The pilot set was fixed at *round-issued* and *@-mention*; it gains **`record-state-changed-by-another-organisation`**, sent to the **producing organisation** when a deliverable of theirs is withdrawn, or superseded by an owner-confirmed cross-document edge (ADR-0013, as amended).

**What it deliberately excludes** is the routine case: when a producer promotes a new version and the prior one supersedes automatically, **the producer caused that itself** and needs telling nothing. The event signals precisely *something you did not do has stopped your deliverable being relied on*.

**Why grow a set that was capped on purpose.** The cap exists to prevent noise, and these acts are **rare by construction** — an owner ending another firm's accepted deliverable is exceptional, not routine — so the volume cost is near zero while the cost of silence is a firm discovering by accident that its work has been set aside. The **content rule is unchanged and load-bearing here**: the email carries the bare fact and a pointer, **never the reason**, because email leaves the platform. The reason is readable in-app, where visibility follows the container it is about (ADR-0013 §4), so an organisation the deliverable was never shared with learns nothing.

**Sequencing, stated rather than discovered.** This rides on T-329 (the record) and T-330 (the emitter), which wait on T-323 and therefore on SES domain verification and production access — external lead time (ADR-0047 §16). The supersede and withdraw *acts* ship first; the telling follows when Slice V1-N lands.

**Amended 2026-10-24 (T-474 and T-475 — ruled by Claude under the owner's delegation of 2026-10-23, the best-in-class build) — a fourth pilot event, the first cross-organisation one, and open gaps join the gate.** *In plain terms: when the owner supersedes or withdraws a library document, every firm whose drafts or past answers relied on it is told — each firm about its own documents only, never about another's. And a draft that still contains a marked gap ("Gap: the record does not give…") cannot be promoted until the gap is resolved, exactly as an open comment or an open finding blocks it today.* **The fourth event is `cited-document-superseded`**, raised when a library version is superseded, withdrawn or re-issued, to each organisation holding a current sandbox document or a past run whose recorded citations point at that version — one notification per organisation, its subject that organisation's own affected document. It joins under decision 2's guard with no schema change. **The cross-organisation visibility the "Open" note said would need stating, stated:** the impact behind this event is computed **per organisation, inside that organisation's own visibility**, and never aggregated — organisation C with nothing citing the document is told nothing and can infer nothing; the owner's own dialog shows library edges and the owner's own work, never that B's private draft cites it. It is the first event the product itself raises (T-330's emitter did not exist; T-474 builds the producer, in-platform; email still waits on Slice V1-N). It is rare by construction — supersession is an owner's exceptional act — so the cap's purpose holds. **Gaps in the gate (decision 7):** an open **gap marker** in a draft counts with open threads and open findings as a promotion block; "threads and findings are treated identically" becomes "threads, findings and gaps"; the override record names the gap count beside the other two. A gap resolves by editing the sentence or deleting the marker — it is text, so it is the author's to clear.

**Open — not decided here.** **Lock-flipped**, and any later event type, join under decision 2's guard. **Whether a notification is ever deleted or expires** rides with `Q-015`'s retention theme rather than being answered on the way past. **Digest and preference behaviour** waits for a real complaint. **Whether the owner organisation sees notifications about a producing organisation's internal round** is not raised by the pilot set — an internal round is participant-private under ADR-0032 and decision 3 resolves it by construction — but it will need stating explicitly the moment a cross-organisation event type is added.
