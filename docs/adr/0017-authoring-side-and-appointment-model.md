# ADR-0017 - The authoring side mirrors the review side: lead author and authors; both sides named on the deliverable plan, inherited at cycle launch, and amendable at any time, each side only over its own set

**Status:** Accepted. Refines ADR-0008, ADR-0014 and ADR-0016. Refined by ADR-0019 (authors may span the organisations sharing the deliverable's sandbox). Refined by ADR-0033 (2026-07-18): both sides are stored as append-only interval grant rows on the container (`deliverable_author` / `deliverable_reviewer`) — inherit-at-launch collapses to the cycle reading the live sets; reviewers may be *proposed* by the producing organisation, conferring no access until owner-confirmed; past-round attribution is reconstructed from intervals, never from snapshots.

**Context:** ADR-0014 handed the edit lock to "the consultant team" at issue without saying who they are or who appoints them, and ADR-0016 said the owner grants all access. A deliverable needs a producing side with the same shape as the reviewing side: people who write it and respond to comments, and one person who holds the authority to promote, which transmits the version and flips the edit lock. We also had to decide who names these people and when.

**Decision:** The producing side mirrors the reviewing side. A deliverable has a lead author and authors, opposite the lead reviewer and reviewers. Authors write the deliverable and its responses; the lead author holds the producing-side transmittal authority, promoting each version, which opens the next review round and flips the edit lock (ADR-0014). Authors belong to the organisations that share the deliverable's sandbox, normally just the producing organisation but possibly several when the sandbox is shared (ADR-0019); reviewers may be cross-organisation.

Both sides are named on the deliverable plan. Each deliverable row names its review group (reviewers and lead reviewer) and its authoring team (authors and lead author). The system inherits both when the review cycle is launched, which is when the lead author promotes the deliverable.

The named sets are defaults, not frozen. Either side may amend its own set at any time, including mid-cycle, because teams change between and within versions; someone goes on leave and a replacement must be added to keep working the DRR. The owner amends the review group, because cross-organisation membership is the owner's domain; the lead author amends the authoring team within the organisations sharing the deliverable's sandbox, with the promote interface as one entry point. Neither side can touch the other's set. A membership change governs who may act in the current and future rounds only; it never alters past rounds, whose comments and responses stay attributed and frozen (ADR-0009, ADR-0014). This follows the standing principle: the owner controls access between organisations, and each lead distributes it to named people within its own organisation, always inside the organisation's owner-granted scope (ADR-0015).

**Consequences:** "The consultant team" of ADR-0014 is the authoring team, and the promoter is specifically the lead author, refining ADR-0016's statement that any member may promote: the producing organisation holds the capability and its lead author is the named hand. ADR-0016's "owner grants all access" is refined: the owner grants cross-organisation access, while intra-organisation distribution is delegated to each side's lead, exercisable at any time. ADR-0008's plan now carries the authoring team alongside the review group, and both are amendable through the cycle rather than fixed at planning. The two sides are symmetric, which keeps the model easy to reason about. Builds on ADR-0005, ADR-0008, ADR-0014, ADR-0015 and ADR-0016.
