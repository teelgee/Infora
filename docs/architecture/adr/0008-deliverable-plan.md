# ADR-0008 - The deliverable plan: planned rows are empty containers from day one; promotion is a planned-container hand-off, not a metadata gate

**Status:** Accepted. Refined by ADR-0017 (the plan also names the authoring team; named sets are amendable at any time). Refined by ADR-0033 (2026-07-18): the plan is **per-producing-organisation** (the TIDP) — each organisation plans, creates and metadatas its own containers and proposes reviewers where known; the owner consolidates all plans into the single project schedule (the MIDP view), curates access and confirms reviewer appointments. "The plan" below reads as that per-org structure plus the owner's consolidation.

**Context:** At project start a consultant and client agree a schedule of deliverables (a Task Information Delivery Plan, TIDP), rolling up into the client's Master Information Delivery Plan (MIDP). Each deliverable's coded reference is generated from the project's naming rules, and its reviewers are known in advance. We previously modelled promotion as a gate where the consultant supplies metadata, naming and reviewers in the moment, which risked fast-but-wrong click-through and left reviewer appointment timing unclear.

**Decision:** The component holds a deliverable plan: a schedule (table) of all deliverables to be produced, which may evolve and be added to. Each planned row is a real container created at planning time, not a reserved string. At creation the row holds its coded reference (generated from the project's naming rules), its named review group and authoring team, its planned metadata, and a status of not-yet-produced. The naming scheme is per project and is not solved here; the component holds whatever scheme a project defines and applies it when a container is created. Drafting happens in the sandbox; the consultant then drops the finished work into the waiting container and promotes it, which sets the container's status into the review cycle (ADR-0005) against the review group already named on the row. Metadata and naming come from the plan, not from promotion. The review group and authoring team also come from the plan, are inherited when the review cycle is launched, and may be amended at any time thereafter, each by its own side: the owner the review group, the lead author the authoring team.

**Consequences:** Promotion becomes a near-empty hand-off rather than a metadata gate, so the click-through-accuracy risk largely disappears: metadata comes from the plan, not from promotion. Reviewers and authors are named at planning and inherited at review cycle launch; each side may amend its own set at any time, including mid-cycle; they are not chosen at transmittal. The plan and the status tracker are the same object: an empty planned container gives the client a live MIDP-style view of every deliverable that should exist and its real state (not produced, in review, accepted). The plan is a new first-class thing in the component, distinct from a deliverable. The per-project reference schema is deferred. Builds on ADR-0004 (PDF review), ADR-0005 (assurance cycle) and ADR-0007 (DRR access).

**Addendum (owner-ruled 2026-08-22, DMS walkthrough session 2): the review group is reconfirmed at promotion.** "Reviewers and authors … are not chosen at transmittal" is amended: promotion now opens a window in which the lead author reconfirms — and may amend — the review group, the lead reviewer included. The mechanism and its limits belong to **ADR-0033's addendum of the same date**; what belongs here is why this does not reopen *"not a metadata gate"*. The window **shows** the plan-owned identity metadata read-only (ADR-0038 §2) and takes input only on authoring metadata and appointments, so the failure this ADR feared — a form filled fast and wrong in the moment — has nothing to bite on: a thoughtless confirm produces exactly the plan's state. And it *reconfirms* rather than composes, which earns its place at round two more than at round one: six months and a staffing change later, the alternative to showing the review group is silently inheriting one named at planning and never looked at since.

> **Addendum (2026-10-24, T-481 — ruled by Claude under the owner's delegation of 2026-10-23, the best-in-class build; milestones on the plan row, and the coverage view).** *Plainly: a plan row can now carry dates — "this document should be at S3, suitable for review and comment, by 30 November" — set by the firm producing it. The client reads them on its schedule beside a count of where every planned document stands: not started, a draft linked, in review, accepted. A date that has passed without the document getting there is marked overdue and shows on the client's home page. The plan can also be downloaded as a spreadsheet in the same shape it is uploaded in, now with a Milestones column.*
>
> **1. A milestone is a target date and a status code, on the plan row.** A row may carry several, one per status code (unique per row and code), stored beside the container — the row's record half — as `plan_milestones` (drawn in [data-model.md](../../build/data-model.md) drawer 2). The code is one of the project's own status-code list (the schema field ADR-0041 names; the seeded list follows the UK National Annex: S0–S7, A1, B1, CR), refused otherwise. Reference material, which has no work item, carries none.
>
> **2. Set by the producing organisation, read by the owner.** The producer's plan editors — the same people who edit the row's metadata (the producer's super user or the row's live lead author, T-362) — add, move and remove milestones; the owner reads them on the consolidated schedule and does not set them. Nobody else reads them: a third organisation the owner shares a row with sees the document, not the producer's schedule for it. This is this ADR's split as ADR-0033 refined it: each organisation plans its own rows (the TIDP), the owner consolidates (the MIDP). A milestone is plan metadata: amendable at any time, like the review group and the authoring team, and it stores who last set it and when — not a history of every reschedule (not taken: a baseline-and-forecast record; revisit if a pilot owner asks "when did this slip?").
>
> **3. Met and overdue are derived when read, never stored.** A milestone is **met** when the row's current status code is its code, or one listed after it in the project's status-code list (the National Annex lists them in the order a document progresses, so a row at A1 has met its S3 milestone). A row whose deliverable has been **accepted** (its review cycle, or its work item, says so) has met every milestone, whatever its status-code field says, because acceptance is the end the codes lead to and the field is typed by hand. It is **overdue** when its date has passed and it is not met. There is no clock and no job — the T-473 pattern. The list order is taken as the project gives it; in the seeded list B1 (partially authorised) sits after A1, so a row at B1 counts as having met an A1 milestone — a project that disagrees reorders its list.
>
> **4. The coverage view counts only what the reader may already see.** Each plan row falls in one stage: planned (no draft bound), draft bound, frozen, QA accepted, promoted, in review, accepted. The internal QA stages — frozen and QA accepted — are the producing organisation's private machinery (ADR-0032), so they are counted for the reader's **own** organisation's rows only; on another organisation's row they fold into "draft bound". One fact is new to the owner, and deliberately: **whether a draft has been bound to a row at all** — the ticket's "no draft bound yet" flag, and the point of the view. It is a fact about progress, never the draft, its title or its content, and nothing in ADR-0032 or ADR-0038 keeps it private (binding joins a draft to a row the owner already reads). *(Corrected in review the same day: the first text said the plan read already showed binding through the work item's status; it does not — binding never moves that status.)*
>
> **5. Overdue milestones reach the work queue** (T-467's "waiting on you"): the owner organisation's people who read the schedule, and the producing organisation's plan editors for their own rows.
>
> **6. The spreadsheet shapes gain the column, and the import stays create-only.** The plan's template export, its batch import (T-461's round-trip shape) and a new whole-plan export carry one **Milestones** column, written `S3 2026-11-30; A1 2027-01-15`. The import still creates rows and never amends an existing one (unchanged); a malformed milestone cell, or a code outside the project's list, is refused in words exactly as the import already refuses any other bad cell (T-359's whole-file verdict: nothing is created until every row is good).
>
> **Personal data:** the milestone's setter is a named participant — registered in [personal-data.md](../personal-data.md); the link is cleared when the person is removed and the milestone stays.
