The returns app, seen twice

One app — the sustainability returns app built in drawing 1 — opened by two people. A contractor's site lead sees a form and their own organisation's returns, nothing else. The client's engineer sees every organisation's returns, the totals, the latest insight, and the accept-or-reject act. Neither screen has a setting that produced the difference: the app shows each person exactly what that person could already see.

Pending — this screen does not exist yet. Drawn for T-438 against ADR-0056 (§2, §2a, §3, §4, §8) and drawer 9 of data-model.md. The screens themselves are what the builder's agent writes (T-442), over the substrate of T-439–T-441; photographed into the walkthrough's app-builder tool section by T-447, at which point this file is deleted (docs/design/README.md). Pattern it starts from (T-437, app-builder-ui-review.md): the client's seat starts from Retool's user mode — the app with no editor chrome, the last published version, and a Use permission that cannot open the editor — and the accept act from Infora's own checker (one finding at a time); the contractor's seat has no product pattern, because neither Retool nor Lovable has a row that belongs to the organisation that entered it (§3 and §5 of the review). Lovable's “Edit with Lovable” badge is the anti-pattern: nothing on either screen advertises the builder. Names: the seeded Wrenfield–Ashcombe project; the seeded world has consultants, not contractors, so Calder & Hale and Redgate Ecology stand in as the submitting organisations. The app's rows and figures are illustrative.

Notes — what is settled, and what rides here as an open fork
Settled (ADR-0056). Same app, two people, no switch between them: each screen calls Infora as its viewer, so Nadia reads Redgate's rows and Rachel reads every organisation's (§4). Accepting is the moment the row changes hands (§2a); the accepted row shows who decided and when, and a rejected one is superseded by a new row. The insight is one row per run with its date, trigger, rows covered and definition version (§3). Export is a file a person issues (§8), stamped with what data and when.
Fork — should the client see a contractor's draft? Drawn: yes, quietly ("Draft — not yet submitted"), because the rule as ruled reads a row by its organisation and by the owner, and drafts are rows. A contractor may reasonably expect a draft to be private until submitted — that is how the sandbox treats unfinished work. Hiding drafts from the owner until submission would be a dated amendment to §2. Recommendation: hide them.
Fork — a reason on rejection. Drawer 9 as drawn carries no reason on a rejection. Screen 1 therefore shows "Rejected" and nothing else, and the contractor learns why some other way. The document review record gives every code a response; a returns app that does not may feel arbitrary to the submitting side. One nullable text column if the owner wants it.
Fork — does the contractor see any project-wide figure? Drawn: no. The insight row is stamped with Trentmoor Energy (the run acted as Rachel) and is read by Trentmoor alone unless a person issues it (§3, §8). If the owner wants contractors to see, say, the project average, that is either an export a person sends or a second insight the contractor's own organisation runs over its own rows.
Fork — accept inline, or in a dialog. Drawn inline: selecting a submitted row opens a confirm strip under the table, on the pattern of the checker's accept-one-at-a-time cards. A modal is the alternative and matches the review cycle's accept dialog. Either way the act is one row at a time, never "accept all".
Who decides, as drawn. Anyone at Trentmoor Energy who may open the dashboard, per the builder's step-4 choice in drawing 1. The decision is recorded in the decider's name (drawer 9: decided by, decided at) — a named-person fact already registered in personal-data.md at ruling.
Totals count accepted rows only. A tile over submitted-but-undecided figures would let a contractor move a project total before the client agreed the number. The insight's "rows covered" is the same set. Drawn that way; worth confirming.
Run now, export, edit the app. "Run the analysis now" is the manual trigger that stands in for the schedule until a server stays on (§13d). "Export…" issues a file carrying only what Rachel may see. "Edit the app" returns to drawing 1 for the app's owner or another permitted builder — nobody else sees it.
Not drawn, on purpose. The Tools card for this app, the list of all runs, the share-a-table dialog, the ownerless "defunct" state, and the playbook shelf — all follow the kit and T-438's entry excludes them. Rail glyphs stand in for the Lucide icons the shell uses.
