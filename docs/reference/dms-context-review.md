# The library, the sandboxes and the context layer against best in class — a review, 2026-10-23

**What this is, in plain terms.** You asked for a review of the part of Infora that decides what counts as true on a project, and where people work before something becomes true: the project library and its register, the deliverable plan, the sandboxes teams draft in, the review cycle, and the context layer that decides what the AI may read and cite. You asked three things: what best in class looks like in construction and engineering, what would have to change in the architecture, and what would have to change on the screens and in the daily workflow to make the product fast, clear and pleasant to use.

**How it was done.** Eight research passes on 2026-10-23 read the incumbent document-control systems, the enterprise AI context layers, the construction-specific AI tools, the interface patterns of adjacent professional software, and then, at your direction the same day, the 2025–2026 AI-native startup cohort and the everyday tools a pilot user already has open; the evidence is in [dms-context-landscape.md](dms-context-landscape.md) and every claim about another product below points into it. In parallel, Infora's own screens and code were inventoried as they stood on the branch `demo/product-videos` at 778fc6d, and the walkthrough screenshots were read. `main` had by then moved on to c34a40c with T-448 to T-454 (the agent-led app builder, the WOPI host and the Collabora editing pane in the sandbox); the library, plan, review and context screens this review covers were re-checked against `main` on 2026-10-23 before any build started, and the few differences are noted where they matter. Every claim about Infora below names the file or screen it rests on.

**What this file is for, and is not.** It is context for decisions, not a decision. Nothing here changes an ADR; the record changes only through the design sessions proposed in §5, with you ruling. Recommendations are numbered R-01 to R-20 so that later agents can amend ADRs, draw mockups and cut tickets against them in a coordinated way (R-19 and R-20 came from the second sweep). **No question, ticket or ADR number is minted here**; whoever picks one up derives the number with the `--next` guards, as the working rules require.

**How to read it.** §1 is the verdict on one page. §2 is the table to review quickly. §3 is the detail per recommendation, written for the agents who will act on it. §4 lists what was looked at and deliberately not recommended. §5 is the plan for turning this into rulings and tickets. §6 is what this review could not verify.

## 1. The verdict on one page

**Infora's foundations are ahead of the market, and its screens are behind it.**

**Where Infora already leads.** Measured against the forty-seven practices in the landscape's §5, Infora already does several things no benchmarked product does, and does them in the database rather than in a policy document:

- **Private knowledge steers but never grounds a cited claim.** No product in any family separates "may steer" from "may ground" (landscape §2, §6 item 7). Infora's channel rule is unique.
- **A document's currency is a record state with a reason, and the AI's reach is derived from it, never set by hand.** The incumbents either cannot undo supersession (Aconex) or have nothing (Microsoft); only Glean has a verify-and-deprecate loop, and it does not say whether deprecation changes retrieval (§6 item 10). Infora's record state plus the acceptance code's grounding flag, with the "this will exclude one live deliverable from AI answers" dialog before the change is saved, is best in class.
- **The wall is enforced in the database for three organisations on one project**, with agents acting as the requester. Every AI tool in the sector is one firm, one tenant (§3, §6 item 13).
- **Citations resolve to a passage offset, and the run record keeps what each answer drew on at passage grain.** Document-level citation is the market norm; Microsoft's own forum says most Copilot answers carry a wrong citation (§2.1).
- **The plan decides first.** References are generated, never invented; binding and promotion reconfirm rather than ask. This is the ProjectWise-style discipline the ISO guidance asks for, without the "Select No Wizard" ritual.
- **Review on an immutable rendition with structured anchors, two gates by two parties mapped to the 2021 National Annex, and the QA checker as typed findings with a coverage statement.** Nomic's "potential" label and Procore's requirement-versus-status table are the nearest the sector comes.

**Where Infora lags.** Three gaps, and they are the gaps a pilot user will feel on day one:

1. **The library is not yet a register.** It is a fixed-order list with no search, no filters, no saved views, no bulk actions, no versions list, no way to see who can see a document, and no way to add material to it from a screen. Reference material has no title on its row and arrives only by an operator's command line. Every incumbent, however dated, has a searchable register with bulk edit (§1); Procore and Asite have saved views; Box and Glean have an intake and a currency loop (§2). This is the single largest source of clunkiness.
2. **The user cannot see or shape what the AI reached.** The scope control is a checkbox menu without search; the ontology's semantic pre-filter is built but nothing can select it (`Q-028`); the citation chip opens the whole document, not the passage; authority citations are not clickable; attaching a source means typing a raw internal id. The market shares the first failing (nobody shows what was out of reach, §6 item 8), which makes it a differentiator Infora is positioned to own, not a gap to close quietly.
3. **The working experience is a set of correct parts without the connective tissue that makes software feel fast.** Four different partial views of one document (plan modal, library modal, review status page, rail drilldown); a lifecycle shown in fragments with different words on each screen; a "⌘K" hint wired to nothing; long explanatory paragraphs under every heading; six disabled placeholder chips in the composer; built capability with no door (sandbox history and restore, library removal, rulebook loading, access grants all exist in the API and not on any screen).

**What the second sweep added.** Sixty-odd more products, from the newest YC companies to the tools on every reviewer's desk, confirmed the shape: review dominates, drafting a whole deliverable is rare and single-firm, and nobody has a wall between organisations (landscape §8.7). It also found a handful of rules worth adopting outright, which strengthen two cards and add two: a finding with no citation is not a finding, and a second pass should try to disprove each one (R-14); a citation is extracted from the source, never generated, and a scan that cannot be cited says so (R-05); a draft shows a visible gap where the record does not answer, and every passage carries who drafted and who accepted it (R-19); and the owner can read, in one table, what each AI feature reads, where it runs and how to switch it off (R-20).

**The shape of the fix.** One structural move, one differentiating move, and one polish slice:

- **Structural:** one document record with one page, one register grammar (search, filters, saved views, side panel, bulk actions) used by the plan, the library and the sandbox alike, one lifecycle vocabulary, doors for what is already built, and the AI transparency table (R-01, R-02, R-09, R-17, R-18, R-20).
- **Differentiating:** a reach statement on every answer, a scope control that is searchable and saveable, citations that land on the passage, supersede-impact that follows a change into every draft that cited the old version, a sandbox working set the AI may read but never cite, and visible gaps with passage authorship (R-03 to R-07, R-19).
- **Working together:** the plan as the owner's schedule with milestones, the home page as a work queue, sandbox presence and history, and review threads and comparison (R-08, R-11, R-12, R-13).
- **Polish, no rulings needed:** the command palette, the findings filters, the copy and vocabulary, and undo (R-10, R-14 except its disproving pass, R-15, R-16).

Three design sessions and one build slice would carry it (§5). None of it touches the wall, the channel rule, record state, or the plan-first discipline. Those stay as ruled.

## 3. The recommendations in detail

Each card has the same parts. **Why** gives the evidence: what Infora does today, with the file or screen, and what the benchmark does, with the landscape section. **What changes** says it in plain words. **Record** names the ADRs, terms and documents that move, and whether a mockup is needed. **Build** is the ticket-shaped outline. **Must not break** names the ruled things the work has to leave alone. **To mint** is the open question, if any, in the form the grill tracker takes.

### R-01 — One document record, one page

**Why.** A deliverable is shown in four partial places today: the plan row's modal (`apps/web/src/screens/plan.tsx:306-560`: title, team, classification, remove), the library row's modal (`library.tsx:90-177`: the rendition only), the review status page (`deliverable-status.tsx`: appointment, reviewers, facts, versions by round, transmittals) and the rail drilldown's plain-text modal (`project.tsx:114-135`). None shows the version history outside a review, none shows who can see the document, and the library shows only the current revision. The benchmark pattern is one record with one stable identity reachable from everywhere (landscape §1.1 Aconex History, §4.4 GitHub, the friction map's C1/C2). The ISO digest's own point applies: state is metadata, not location, so one page can show every state.

**What changes.** A container gets one page. Its tabs: **Overview** (title, reference, producer, the lifecycle badge pair from R-09, classification, who can see it, the actions the viewer's role allows), **Versions** (every working, preliminary and contractual revision with its rendition links and, for authored documents, a compare control), **Relationships** (the ontology panel, moved here from the plan modal), **Review record**, **Transmittals** and **Activity** (the audit trail the viewer is entitled to). The plan row, the library row, the review list row, a citation chip's "open document" and the rail drilldown all land here. The three modals and the rail's text modal retire. Quick edits (title, team, classification) move to the side panel of R-02 so the page is for reading the record and the panel is for changing a field.

**Record.** Component 5's surfaces list (`docs/architecture/components/component-5-document-management.md`, §§1, 2, 8, 9) gains "the container page" as the surface the four capabilities share; no ADR changes, because access is unchanged (per deliverable, per organisation, ADR-0016) and the page only shows what the access model already resolves. A mockup is needed before building (design README lifecycle). `CONTEXT.md` may want the term **Document page** if the sessions find the screens need a name.

**Build.** (1) Route `/projects/:id/documents/:containerId` with the six tabs reading existing endpoints; (2) Versions tab over the versions table with renditions; (3) Relationships tab hosting `ontology-panel.tsx`; (4) retarget every link and chip; (5) retire the modals; (6) walkthrough beats re-photographed. Acceptance: from any screen a document is one click from its page; the page shows the same facts to the owner and the producer save for the actions each may take; the wall negative (a member organisation outside the share sees a 404, not a page).

**Must not break.** Per-deliverable visibility and the owner's control of sharing (ADR-0016, 0019); DRR access phases (ADR-0014); the rule that a sandbox-scope review record never crosses the wall (ADR-0032).

### R-02 — The register grammar

**Why.** The library has no search, filters, sorting, pagination, selection, bulk actions or saved views (`library.tsx`, inventory §1.1); the only register search is the plan's text box over reference and title (`plan.tsx:836-842`); the sandbox has no search across documents. Rows carry five inline text actions that wrap and truncate at the walkthrough width ("Supersede…", "Withdraw" cut off in `26-library.png`). Reference material rows have no title column. Meanwhile the hybrid retrieval index (ADR-0022) already searches the library by meaning and by code for the agent, and people cannot use it. The benchmark: every incumbent has a searchable register with bulk attribute edit (ACC §1.2, Asite §1.4), Procore and Asite ship saved views shareable by link (§1.6, §1.4), Linear's filters belong to the view and work from the keyboard (§4.2), and the Nielsen Norman Group's guidance on tables and bulk actions is explicit about a contextual action bar with undo (§4.5).

**What changes.** One table grammar, used by the plan, the library, the sandbox's all-documents view and the review list: a search box at the top that searches reference, title and content through the same index the agent uses, with a visible "filtered" state; filter chips by discipline, document type, status code, revision, record state, context state, producer, reviewer and date; a column chooser (the plan's organisation-standard columns stay locked as ruled; personal views may hide, not add); saved views with a name, shareable within the organisation by link; row selection with a bulk-action bar (transmit, issue externally, propose supersede, set classification with an explicit append-or-replace choice, export the selection); and a side panel on row select for the quick edits now in modals. Row actions collapse to one menu. Reference material gets a title and the same columns. Sort on any column.

**Record.** Design rules (`docs/design/design-rules.md` §B) gain four components: Side panel, Bulk-action bar, Filter chip, Saved view. `CONTEXT.md` gains **Saved view** (a named filter over a register, private to its maker or shared within the organisation, never an access object) so nobody later mistakes it for a folder. ADR-0016's presentation note already says folders organise and never grant; a saved view is the same kind of thing and the term should say so. ADR-0022 gains a consequence note that the library index serves people as well as agents, access-resolved identically. A mockup first.

**Build.** (1) A `Register` component in `packages/ui` with the grammar above; (2) a `GET /projects/:id/library/search` endpoint over the retrieval pre-filter, returning rows not chunks, access-resolved; (3) saved views as a small table (owner participant, organisation, name, filter JSON); (4) bulk endpoints where only single-row ones exist (transmit several, classify several); (5) the plan, library, sandbox and review list moved onto the component; (6) register export to Excel. Acceptance: a document controller finds any document by meaning or code in one box; a filtered view survives reload; a bulk transmit of twenty rows is one dialog; the wall negative (a saved view shared across the wall is impossible by construction because it is per organisation).

**Must not break.** The organisation-standard locked column set on the plan (component 5 §1, 2026-08-09 ruling); access per deliverable; the audit of every state action.

### R-03 — Reference material intake and currency

**Why.** Reference material reaches the library only through an operator's command line (`apps/api/src/run-bulk-load.ts`; inventory §6); the owner has no upload control on the library; a loaded document is "reference" because it has no work item, and nothing can re-mark it. Loaded reference rows show reference, revision, held by and loaded, with no title, no classification and no currency (`library.tsx:966-1017`). Classification is manual at pilot scale by ruling, and the agent-proposed half is deferred (plan.md, ontology slice O part 2). The benchmark: Box's intake pipeline (AI proposes domain, tags, summary and a health score; a human approves and names an owner; stale items are re-flagged, §2.4), Glean's "verified by whom, when" with a re-check reminder and a deprecate action (§2.2), ACC's required attributes at upload (§1.2). The ISO digest's required metadata (revision, status, classification assigned separately from the ID) applies to reference material as much as to deliverables.

**What changes.** The owner uploads reference material from the library screen, in bulk, with progress. Each file lands in an **intake queue**: the AI proposes a title, a document type, a discipline and the other vocabulary facets, with its confidence shown as a word not a number; the owner corrects and confirms, singly or in bulk, before the document enters the grounded corpus. Each reference row then carries **confirmed current by** and **re-check due** (a date the owner sets, with a sensible default per document type, such as a standard's edition cycle). A due re-check puts the document in the owner's work queue (R-11). A reference document nobody has confirmed current is shown as such on its row and, by the ruling asked for below, either keeps grounding with that fact shown on every citation of it, or stops grounding until confirmed.

**Record.** ADR-0011 addendum: reference material's lifecycle (upload, intake, confirmation, re-check) and who may do each (the owner organisation's super user, as curation already is). ADR-0045: the agent-proposed classification deferral is split, and the reference-material half pulled forward (the deliverable half stays deferred, because a deliverable's classification rides its plan row). ADR-0013 or a new question: whether "awaiting re-check" is a record state, a flag that does not touch grounding, or a flag that does. `docs/architecture/personal-data.md` gains the confirmer's name on the reference row. The data model (drawer 2) draws the intake row and the two currency columns before any migration. A mockup of the intake queue first.

**Build.** (1) Upload endpoint for reference material reusing the bulk-load path, with the operator CLI kept for the one-off 500-document load; (2) intake table and proposal run (the ontology write path already carries `proposedByRunId`, which today nothing populates); (3) the intake screen with confirm, correct and bulk-confirm; (4) currency columns and the re-check job; (5) the citation badge if the ruling says so. Acceptance: fifty PDFs dropped on the library become fifty titled, classified rows after one review pass; nothing is grounded on before confirmation; the audit names who confirmed what.

**Must not break.** Acceptance remains the only way a deliverable enters the library (ADR-0011); the ontology stays agent-proposed and human-confirmed (ADR-0045); deletion stays the owner organisation's act (ADR-0050).

**To mint.** *The user situation:* a British Standard the owner loaded two years ago has a new edition. Nobody has withdrawn the old one. Does the AI keep citing it with a visible "not confirmed current since 2024" mark, or does it stop citing it until someone confirms? Recommendation: keep grounding, mark every citation, and put the re-check in the owner's queue, because silence is worse than a dated answer and withdrawal already exists for the case where the owner knows it is wrong. Stress test: a reviewer sees "not confirmed current" on every citation of a standard that is in fact current, and learns to ignore the mark.

### R-04 — The reach statement and the scope control

**Why.** The ontology's semantic pre-filter is built and nothing can select it; every run scans the whole access-resolved library (`Q-028`, measured at 133 ms to 6 ms on a 20,000-chunk project). The scope control in Ask is a checkbox menu of every document with no search (`composer-chips.tsx:49-122`); the document creator has the same list (`tools.tsx:1167-1198`); the checker alone has search boxes and copy saying which choice yields citations (`tools.tsx:1288-1326`). An answer says how many claims were uncited and what was withheld, but not what was in reach and what was excluded. The benchmark says nobody shows what was out of reach, and Microsoft admits the answer simply gets thinner (§2.1, §6 item 8); Harvey and Notion let the user scope by chosen sources and pin context with @-mentions (§4.1, §4.2); Nomic confirms scope before a large review (§3.1). Infora can do what they cannot, because it pre-filters in SQL and records the plan.

**What changes.** One **scope control** used by Ask, the document creator, the checker and the editor chat: a searchable picker with facets from the classification vocabulary (discipline, document type, work package, phase), explicit documents, and saved scopes with a name. The chosen scope is written on the run's plan before retrieval and shown on the run. Every answer and every draft carries a **reach statement** in plain words: "Read 14 documents in this project's library within your scope. Excluded: 2 superseded, 1 withdrawn, 1 awaiting re-check. Your scope left out 31 documents you could have included." It never counts what the wall hides from the requester, because a count of unshared documents is itself a disclosure (ADR-0018, `Q-029`). When a scope leaves nothing to read, the run says so before it spends (ADR-0053's coverage rule already does this for the checker).

**Record.** ADR-0029 addendum: the assembly request carries a declared scope (vocabulary terms, container ids, or a saved scope), recorded on the run; this is the mechanism `Q-028` asks for, and the recommendation in the tracker (declared, auditable, pre-retrieval, visible, never implicit) is adopted as written. ADR-0045: the pre-filter is wired. ADR-0018 consequence note: a reach statement may count what the requester may see and what state excluded, never what access excluded. `CONTEXT.md` gains **Scope** (what a run was told to read, declared before retrieval) and **Reach statement** (what it did read and why the rest was left out). A mockup of the control and the statement.

**Build.** (1) `AssemblyRequest.request` gains `scope`; the gateway applies it as the semantic pre-filter; (2) the reach counts come back on the run; (3) the `ScopeControl` component replacing the Data sources chip and the two checkbox lists; (4) saved scopes (per participant, optionally shared within the organisation); (5) the reach statement rendered on Ask answers, drafts and findings. Acceptance: scoping to one discipline on the seeded project is three keystrokes; the statement's numbers match the audit's passage-grain record; the wall negative (a user in organisation B never sees a count that changes when A shares one more document with C).

**Must not break.** Access resolved before retrieval (ADR-0020, 0022); the checker's coverage statement (ADR-0053); the plan as a stored, editable artefact (the agent harness).

**To mint.** None beyond `Q-028`, which this answers, and the disclosure rule, which should be ruled as an ADR-0018 note in the same session.

### R-05 — Citations land on the passage

**Why.** A library citation chip opens the whole document in a side panel (`source-panel.tsx:12-52`); the exact chunk "still waits on the chunk-provenance theme" (file header); authority chips have no click handler (`cited-prose.tsx:42-46`); in the editor the chip opens a centred text modal instead (`editor.tsx:613`). The passage-grain audit already stores the chunk id per citation (T-253), so the data exists. The research is unambiguous: citations raise trust even when wrong, only about a fifth of answers get checked, and a source card beside the claim is noticed in about a second while a bare link takes sixteen (landscape §4.7). Autodesk Assistant highlights and links the spec text; Egnyte and NotebookLM open at the passage (§1.2, §2.5, §2.6).

**What changes.** One **source card** component: hovering a chip shows the cited passage with its document, revision, page and record state; clicking opens the document page (R-01) scrolled to the passage, highlighted. Authority chips do the same against the authority corpus when it lands and, until then, show the reference and the clause. Sentences the ground-check admitted carry a chip; sentences it did not are already badged "includes model knowledge", and that badge becomes per sentence. The same card is used in Ask, the editor, the checker's cited passages and the review record's response links. A quoted rule in a finding is **not** shown through this card: ADR-0048 §5 rules that a quote must render differently from a grounded citation and never resolve like one, so a rule gets its own visibly distinct card (R-14). **Two rules the second sweep makes explicit (landscape §8.6, item 37).** The passage text on the card is always read from the stored chunk the run recorded, never produced by a model: Google's own help admits Gemini "may miss a source it used, cite one it didn't use for a specific claim, or occasionally invent a source", Adobe's community manager says Acrobat drops citations when the question's language differs from the document, and the one published guarantee of valid pointers found (the Claude Citations API) comes from extraction. And a scanned PDF whose text could not be extracted is not citable; the library row, the scope control and the reach statement say so plainly rather than letting a thin answer pass as a complete one.

**Record.** `Q-022` closes with a note on ADR-0022 that a citation renders its passage from the recorded chunk, never re-retrieved. No new terms. A small mockup of the card.

**Build.** (1) A `GET /versions/:id/passages/:chunkId` read returning the passage text and its offsets, access-resolved; (2) the `SourceCard` component; (3) the document page accepts a passage anchor; (4) the chip in `cited-prose.tsx` and the finding card use it; (5) the authority chip gets a handler. Acceptance: hover shows the passage within a second; click lands on it highlighted; a superseded source's card says so.

**Must not break.** Attached sources never emit a citation (ADR-0022); findings quote rules and never cite them (ADR-0048).

### R-06 — Supersede impact

**Why.** Infora is the only product reviewed whose data can answer "what else relied on this": every run records the passages it drew on, every version is immutable, and `references` edges exist (ADR-0045). Today a confirmed `supersedes` edge stops the old document grounding and the plan shows the badge. The **producing** organisation is ruled to be told, by pointer, when its own deliverable is withdrawn or superseded (ADR-0049's 2026-08-27 amendment added that third pilot event; it is ruled and not yet built, T-329 to T-332). Nobody else is told, and nothing finds the drafts and versions that **cited** the old document, which is the larger population. The acceptance-code dialog already shows a blast radius before a change ("This will exclude 1 live deliverable from AI answers", `27a-blast-radius.png`). The benchmark has nothing like it: stale content handling is the market's weakness (§6 item 10). The deferred ontology slice names supersede-impact as a later Engine workflow (plan.md, slice O part 2).

**What changes.** Impact is shown **to each organisation about its own work, never to the owner about anyone else's.** When the owner supersedes, withdraws or re-issues a library document, the dialog shows the owner only what the owner may already see: the library versions whose `references` edges point at it, and the owner's own drafts and runs. On confirmation, every organisation whose drafts, promoted versions or past answers cited the document is told by a notification pointing at its own affected documents, and the editor and the checker raise a **stale grounding** finding on each affected passage: "this sentence cited WA-GEO-GIR-004 v2, which was superseded by v3 on 12 October; re-check against v3". The finding is mechanical where the passage is unchanged in the new revision and judgement where it is not. A run's audit stays visible only within the organisation that ran it, so the impact query runs per organisation and is never aggregated across the wall.

**Record.** ADR-0045: supersede-impact pulled forward from slice O part 2 as a deterministic check over the audit, not an agent workflow (it needs no model to find the citations). ADR-0048 and ADR-0053: a new finding kind, "stale grounding", with its quote rule (it quotes the old and new passage of the library document, which are shareable because they are library material). ADR-0049: a fourth pilot event, "a document you cited was superseded", beside the three already ruled (round issued, @-mention, and record state changed by another organisation); this widens the pilot set and needs your ruling, and the recommendation is to add it because it replaces an email somebody would otherwise send. ADR-0039 §4 and ADR-0018 are **unchanged and load-bearing**: the per-organisation scoping above exists to keep them. `docs/architecture/personal-data.md` unchanged.

**Build.** (1) An impact query over the passage-grain audit joined to current sandbox documents and versions, scoped to one organisation; (2) the owner's dialog panel over library edges and the owner's own work; (3) the notification event; (4) the stale-grounding finding in the checker's plan and in the editor's Checks tab; (5) the document page's Relationships tab shows "cited by" for library versions only. Acceptance: supersede the seeded ground investigation report and the seeded method statement shows a finding on the sentence that cited it; organisation C, which cannot see the method statement, sees nothing; the owner's dialog never changes when organisation B's private draft cites the document.

**Must not break.** Supersession is a confirmed edge, withdrawal a state (ADR-0013); nothing is auto-applied to a person's text (ADR-0048 §3); notification content never leaves the platform (ADR-0049); a run's audit internals and source set are visible only within the producing organisation (ADR-0039 §4); a private project sandbox is reachable by the owner only through the audited override (ADR-0018).

**To mint.** *The user situation:* a consultant's accepted chapter cited a flood study that the owner has now superseded with a different study. The chapter is contractual and accepted. Is the chapter's authoring team told, and does anything on the chapter's record change? Recommendation: told, yes; the chapter's record state unchanged, with the stale citation shown on its page as a fact for the next revision. Stress test: a hundred superseded reference documents on a long programme flood the queue.

### R-07 — The sandbox working set as context

**Why.** The AI never grounds on the sandbox, by ruling, and that stays. But a consultant's daily question is "given these three drafts of mine and the library, what have we not covered?", and today the three surfaces answer it three different ways: the checker has a picker with search over the user's own drafts ("Search your working area", `tools.tsx:1440`, several at once); the editor's attach control asks the user to type a raw internal id (`working-set.tsx:23-71`); Ask has no attach at all, and its Sandbox search chip is a disabled placeholder (`composer-sources.tsx`). `Q-020` asks whether sandbox material may enter chat context at all. Answers that lean on it say "drawing on your working area" and never cite it; the set is visible on the run and in the shared thread (ADR-0039) to the sandbox's participants only. Nothing is automatic: the working set is empty until chosen, and a saved scope (R-04) may include one.

**Record.** `Q-020` ruled by an ADR-0022 addendum: sandbox material may enter a run only as a user-chosen working set, on the attached-source terms (never joins a corpus, never auto-grounds, never cites), scoped to the sandbox's participant set. ADR-0021 unchanged: this is not a fourth channel, it is the attached source made plural. ADR-0039: the shared thread shows the set. `CONTEXT.md` gains **Working set** beside **Attached source**.

**Build.** (1) The checker's picker is lifted into a `WorkingSetPicker` component and `WorkingSet` accepts many documents; (2) the control appears in Ask, the creator and the editor, replacing the id field; (3) the run records the set; (4) the "Sandbox search" placeholder chip is removed (R-15) and replaced by this. **Must not break.** The sandbox never grounds and never cites (ADR-0011, 0022); the thread's visibility is the sandbox's participant set (ADR-0039); the informing channel is never pooled by a shared thread (ADR-0039 §3).

### R-14 — Findings polish, and three rules from the checkers

**Why.** The checker's panel already groups mechanical corrections for one-click accept and reads rewrites one at a time (`t2-08-panel.png`), which is the Grammarly pattern done right (landscape §4.6). It lacks a severity filter with counts (Vale), and a long document's findings have no table view (Harvey's review tables, Legora's grid, §4.1 and legora-ui-review.md). Quoted rules show the clause text inline, with no card of their own; by ADR-0048 §5 they must never look like a citation, so whatever they get has to be visibly a different thing. The second sweep looked at a dozen drawing, spec and contract checkers (landscape §8.2) and three of their habits are ahead of Infora's checker today: **every finding cites both sides** (the spec section and the sheet at Helonic, the drawing page and the code section at UpCodes, the sheet and an evidence snippet at InspectMind); **"no citation to the governing paragraph or sheet, no finding"**, with a second agent that tries to disprove each finding before it is shown (Specset); and a **stated strength map** ("strongest on quantitative cross-references, weakest on intent and tone" at Helonic; accuracy by input quality at Civils.ai). Infora's findings already quote a rule and cite a passage (ADR-0048) and state coverage (ADR-0053); they do not yet open both sides together, do not run a disproving pass, and the tool describes what it reads but not what it is weak at.

**What changes.** Severity and kind filters with counts at the top of the Checks tab; a **table view** of findings for long documents (section, kind, severity, rule, state) that a reviewer can sort and work through; the source card (R-05) on every cited passage, and a separate **rule card** on every quoted rule, styled as a quotation with the rule's origin and clause number and no chip, so that a quote can never be mistaken for a citation (ADR-0048 §5 kept as written); for a finding that rests on two places (the draft's sentence and the record's passage, or a rule and its exception) **both open side by side**; the rule "a judgement finding with no passage and no rule is not raised" stated in the plan and enforced in code; an optional **disproving pass** for judgement findings, run as a second model call per finding that tries to show the finding is wrong, with findings it defeats dropped and counted in the coverage statement; and a **strength map** on the launcher and beside the findings, in the plan's words, saying which rule kinds are settled in code and which rest on a model's reading. Nothing is ever applied without the person.

**Record.** ADR-0053 addendum if the disproving pass is adopted: it is a declared step in the plan with its cost shown before the run, and the coverage statement counts what it removed. ADR-0048 §5 unchanged. The strength map is copy, keyed to the five rule kinds of ADR-0054.

**Build.** Filters and the table toggle (small); the rule card and the two-sided view over R-05's component; the no-citation rule in the finding writer; the disproving step as a planner option; the strength-map copy. Acceptance: forty findings on a long chapter can be narrowed to the required ones in one click; a finding with no rule and no passage cannot exist in the store; the plan shows the disproving step's cost and the coverage statement shows its removals.

**Must not break.** Four rule kinds of five stay settled in code with no model call (ADR-0054); the plan is computed in code and the same draft yields the same plan (ADR-0053); findings quote and never cite a rule (ADR-0048 §5).

### R-19 — Visible gaps, and passage authorship

**Why.** When the drafting agent cannot ground a sentence, Infora today either withholds it or lets it through as model knowledge with an answer-level badge ("includes model knowledge — N uncited claims", `ask.tsx:414-420`); nothing marks the place in the text, and the exported Word file shows nothing at all. The accept and reject acts on suggestions are recorded as Signals (ADR-0039 §1, the suggestion and signal model), but the document carries no visible record of which passages the agent drafted and who accepted them. The second sweep found the two habits that answer this (landscape §8.1, items 36 and 38): Avoice's spec drafter leaves **"TBC"** where the drawings do not answer rather than guessing (vendor claim, via search summary); Alloovium **tracks AI authorship on the drafted document** and finalises nothing without a named human; Catenda keeps anything an AI creates **a draft until a person approves**. The citation research in landscape §4.7 is the reason it matters: a plausible sentence with a citation is trusted even when wrong, so the honest signal is an explicit gap, not a quiet omission.

**What changes.** Where the ground-check could not admit a drafted sentence, the agent's proposed draft carries a **gap marker** in its place, a typed placeholder in the structured text ("Gap: the record does not give the made-ground thickness per parcel"), never prose. It reaches the document the way every drafted passage does, through the Suggestion the person accepts, so no new write path exists. The author resolves it by writing, citing, attaching a source or deleting it; an open gap blocks promotion exactly as an open finding does (ADR-0049's gate), and the Checks tab lists open gaps beside open findings. Every passage carries **authorship provenance**: typed by a person, or drafted by the agent in a named run and accepted by a named person on a date, shown on hover in the editor and in the document page's Activity tab (R-01), and available as an appendix on export so a client reviewer can see which passages were machine-drafted if the project wants that shown.

**Record.** ADR-0048 addendum: the gap marker is the one non-prose element the drafting side may propose, typed as such, and it lands only through an accepted Suggestion. This amends ADR-0048, which does not contemplate a placeholder, but leaves §3 as it stands: every change to a person's text remains a human act, and the checker still gains no write path the drafting side does not have. ADR-0049: open gaps join open threads and open findings as a promotion block, overridable on the same elevated tier. `CONTEXT.md` gains **Gap marker**. `docs/architecture/personal-data.md`: the acceptor's name already rides the Signal; the authorship display reads it, so the register entry is checked rather than added. The data model draws passage-level authorship on the structured text's blocks before any migration.

**Build.** (1) A gap node type in the structured text and its rendering; (2) drafting mode emits gaps where the ground-check withholds; (3) the Checks tab and the promotion gate count open gaps; (4) hover provenance read from Signals and run records; (5) the export appendix as a project option. Acceptance: a draft of the seeded chapter against a library missing the ground model shows a gap at the sentence that needed it; promotion is refused while it is open; hover on any sentence names its author and acceptor.

**Must not break.** Nothing applied to a person's text without the person (ADR-0048 §3); attached sources never emit a citation (ADR-0022); label propagation on write-back; the shared thread's visibility (ADR-0039).

### R-20 — The AI transparency table

**Why.** Infora records per run the model, the cost and the sources (ADR-0028), has deployment security tiers (ADR-0037), a privacy programme (ADR-0050), never trains on customer data (ADR-0026), and will sign a zero-data-retention agreement for the pilot (v1.md). **Build.** A per-project feature-switch table, the page, enforcement at each tool's launcher and gateway entry, the export. Acceptance: switching the checker off on the seeded project removes it from Tools and the editor toolbar and refuses the endpoint; the audit names who switched it and why; the page's rows match the features that exist.

## 4. Looked at, and deliberately not recommended

- **Access per status code** (Asite and ProjectWise, landscape §5 item 1). Infora separates work from the record by sandbox scope and shares the record per deliverable, which is what per-status access is trying to reach by other means. Adding it would reintroduce the folder-shaped partition the wall model rejects.
- **Folders as access objects.** ADR-0016 ruled folders organise and never grant, and the incumbents' forum complaints show why (§1.2).
- **Auto-sharing on add** (Dropbox Dash, §4.5). The opposite of the wall. R-02's bulk actions and R-07's working set always show who will see what before anything moves.
- **A numeric confidence score on answers or findings.** The research says showing confidence lowers trust (§4.7), and Nomic's "potential" word label is the right shape; Infora's withheld and uncited badges already do this work.
- **A content health score as a verdict** (Box, §2.4). R-03 proposes a confirmation and a re-check date instead, because a score nobody owns is a number nobody acts on.
- **A user-facing model picker.** `Q-021` stands; the Model chip is removed under R-15 rather than wired.
- **Figma's all-or-nothing review.** Per-comment outcomes and per-passage accept are already ruled and better.
- **A graph explorer for relationships.** It rides breadth surfaces as ruled; R-01's Relationships tab and R-06's "cited by" count are the useful part at pilot scale.
- **Groovy-style scripting for rules** (Asite, §1.4). The rule set (ADR-0054) and the app builder's playbooks already cover custom enforcement without code.
- **Event-triggered agent runs** (Procore, §1.6). Worth having after V1, and the app builder's playbooks are the home; nothing in R-01 to R-20 needs it.
- **"Bring your own AI": an outside assistant given direct access to project material** (Bluebeam's and Catenda's MCP connections, TestFit, Revizto's announced server, nPlan's connection to Claude and ChatGPT; landscape §8.5, friction 17). It sends document text, markup data and file metadata to an assistant under the user's own permissions. On a walled project that is a leak path: the outside assistant is not inside the assembly environment, cannot be held to the channel rule, and its retention is not Infora's. The ruled shape is the M365 Copilot surface (ADR-0031): a thin client that reaches Infora only through Infora's own API and gateway, whatever the plug-in protocol (the ADR allows OpenAPI or MCP for the plug-in itself). The protocol is not the objection; direct access is.
- **A vendor's own staff inside the loop** (Build.inc, Archistar, Buildcheck as reported, Qbiq; §8.2, §8.4). It is the honest way those products reach acceptable accuracy, and it cannot sit behind a wall: Infora's operator access is break-glass and disclosed on request (ADR-0050), never a review step.
- **Memory across sessions and projects** (UpCodes' Project Memory and User Memory; §8.2). On a walled platform a remembered item could carry one party's material into another's answer. Infora's informing channel is already the firm-scoped memory, by construction; nothing user-scoped should remember across environments.
- **Agents that file on the user's behalf without a review step** (PermitFlow's Submission agent; §8.4). Every write to the record stays a person's act, as the plan-first and acceptance rules already say.

## 5. How to turn this into rulings and tickets

**Three design sessions and one build slice**, in this order. Each session is a comment-directed grill session on the pattern the record already uses: provisional rulings in a session log, repo edits in one reconciled pass at the end, the owner ruling the product forks and the agent defending the mechanics. Each session's mockups are drawn before it opens, so you are ruling on something you can see.

**Session 1, the document record** (R-01, R-02, R-09, R-17, R-18, R-20). Mockups: the document page; the register with side panel and bulk bar; the transparency table. Tickets come out as one slice, with R-17's doors, R-09's badge and R-20's table first because they are small and unblock the rest. R-15 and R-16 can ride the same slice without a ruling.

**Session 2, reach, intake and currency** (R-03, R-04, R-05, R-06, R-07, R-19). Rules: `Q-028`, `Q-020`, `Q-022`, the stale reference question in R-03, the accepted-chapter question in R-06, the reach statement's disclosure rule, whether "a document you cited was superseded" joins the pilot notification set, the gap marker as the one thing the agent may write, and open gaps as a promotion block. Touches ADR-0011, 0018 (note), 0022, 0029, 0045, 0048, 0049, 0053; `CONTEXT.md` (Scope, Reach statement, Working set, Gap marker); personal-data (the confirmer; the acceptor checked); data model drawer 2 (intake, currency, passage authorship). **Session 3, working together** (R-08, R-11, R-12, R-13). Rules: milestones on the plan row; the request-review step; thread states; the response-to-change link; the compare view. Touches ADR-0008 (addendum), component 5 §1 and §3; data model drawer 2 (milestones). Mockups: the coverage view; the history panel and request dialog; the threads panel and compare tab.

**The polish slice, no session** (R-10, R-14 except its disproving pass, R-15, R-16): ticketable now against existing rulings, and the fastest way to remove the clunkiness you named. It is also the safest place to start, because nothing in it changes the record.

**Ordering against the board.** The pilot deployment (slice V1-I) stays ahead of everything here, as the board says. **What the record should carry from this review when it is done.** This file stays in `docs/reference/` as context. The landscape stays as the benchmark. The ProjectWise friction map's index row already says it is superseded as the benchmark. Nothing in the two files should ever be cited by an ADR; the sessions' ADR addenda state the reasoning in their own words.

## 6. What this review could not verify

- **No user was watched.** Every judgement about clunkiness comes from reading the code, the screenshots and the click counts, not from a pilot user's hands. The Nielsen Norman Group's advice in the landscape (§4.7) applies to this review too: test with two or three real document controllers on real documents before Session 1 closes, and let their session change the order above.
- **Review-site complaints about the incumbents are directional.** The pages refused to load and the quotes come from search excerpts (landscape §7).
- **Several benchmark features are announced, not shipped** (Autodesk's Smart Controls and next Assistant, ProjectWise AI's general availability, Procore's agents). Where a recommendation leans on one, the card says so.
- **The walkthrough screenshots are from 2026-09-02** and the inventory is from the code on 2026-10-23; the two agree on every screen named here, but the app-builder work since then was not in scope and is not covered.
- **Click counts** are from the code's control structure, not timed sessions, and the sizes in §2 are estimates for one engineer against the existing packages.
- **The second sweep's evidence is thinner than the first's.** Almost no reviewer quotes were retrievable for the startup cohort, many sites returned only titles or search excerpts, and every accuracy figure is the vendor's own (landscape §7, second block). Two names you gave could not be resolved as described: **Build.inc's "Lenny" was not found anywhere**, and Build's own platform is named "Dougie", so the name may have been heard elsewhere; and **"Avoice"** resolved cleanly to avoice.co, the YC-backed architecture workspace, with no competing match. The Cemex Ventures Top 50 lists would not load and are unswept.
