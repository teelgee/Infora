# ADR-0008 - The deliverable plan: planned rows are empty containers from day one; promotion is a planned-container hand-off, not a metadata gate

**Status:** Accepted. Refined by ADR-0017 (the plan also names the authoring team; named sets are amendable at any time).

**Context:** At project start a consultant and client agree a schedule of deliverables (a Task Information Delivery Plan, TIDP), rolling up into the client's Master Information Delivery Plan (MIDP). Each deliverable's coded reference is generated from the project's naming rules, and its reviewers are known in advance. We previously modelled promotion as a gate where the consultant supplies metadata, naming and reviewers in the moment, which risked fast-but-wrong click-through and left reviewer appointment timing unclear.

**Decision:** The component holds a deliverable plan: a schedule (table) of all deliverables to be produced, which may evolve and be added to. Each planned row is a real container created at planning time, not a reserved string. At creation the row holds its coded reference (generated from the project's naming rules), its named review group and authoring team, its planned metadata, and a status of not-yet-produced. The naming scheme is per project and is not solved here; the component holds whatever scheme a project defines and applies it when a container is created. Drafting happens in the sandbox; the consultant then drops the finished work into the waiting container and promotes it, which sets the container's status into the review cycle (ADR-0005) against the review group already named on the row. Metadata and naming come from the plan, not from promotion. The review group and authoring team also come from the plan, are inherited when the review cycle is launched, and may be amended at any time thereafter, each by its own side: the owner the review group, the lead author the authoring team.

**Consequences:** Promotion becomes a near-empty hand-off rather than a metadata gate, so the click-through-accuracy risk largely disappears: metadata comes from the plan, not from promotion. Reviewers and authors are named at planning and inherited at review cycle launch; each side may amend its own set at any time, including mid-cycle; they are not chosen at transmittal. The plan and the status tracker are the same object: an empty planned container gives the client a live MIDP-style view of every deliverable that should exist and its real state (not produced, in review, accepted). The plan is a new first-class thing in the component, distinct from a deliverable. The per-project reference schema is deferred. Builds on ADR-0004 (PDF review), ADR-0005 (assurance cycle) and ADR-0007 (DRR access).
