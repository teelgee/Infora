# ADR-0032 - The review cycle generalised: one workflow at two scopes; the cycle is the instance and the DRR is its record; sandbox versioning and the freeze

**Status:** Accepted (owner-ruled 2026-07-18, Slice A design session). Refines ADR-0002, 0003, 0004, 0005, 0009, 0013, 0014, 0017. Drawn in [diagrams/slice-a-review-model.tldr](../architecture/diagrams/slice-a-review-model.tldr). **Refined 2026-07-22 (producing-side-lifecycle grill), sandbox-scope cycle mechanics:** an internal cycle runs in a private *or* shared sandbox; **initiation is open to the author or anyone in the team** (distinct from the project-scope cycle, which the lead author launches by promoting, ADR-0017); the initiator **names the review participants at initiation, and that named set — a subset of the sandbox participant set — is who holds DRR access** (the internal DRR's grain is the cycle's named participants, participant-private per ADR-0036, never the client); **the freeze is automatic at initiation** (starting the cycle snapshots and locks the head, no separate manual step) and **the head unlocks when the round closes**, ready for the team to edit and respond to the DRR.

**Context:** ADRs 0005–0014 describe the review cycle as consultant-vs-client, and Drawer 5 modelled one container-level DRR with no cycle, round or transmittal tables. But teams review in three ways before anything reaches a client: informal check-my-text markup, QA review ending in an issue-ready version, and a formal cycle with suggested-changes-only and a kept record (an internal management plan needs the full ceremony without ever crossing the wall). The sandbox is meant to mimic how teams work in SharePoint — good ambient versioning, and check-out when a document must hold still. And rejection needed a real pathway: a rejected document may be resubmitted, or may stop dead and remain on the record.

**Decision:** The review cycle is **role-agnostic and scope-parameterised**: every cycle instance has an authoring side and a reviewing side, and runs at one of two scopes — **sandbox scope** (org-private: the internal formal cycle; its record never crosses the wall and is retained permanently as QA evidence) or **project scope** (the wall-crossing client cycle, ADR-0014 visibility unchanged). Internal QA and client review are the same workflow.

The sandbox gains **ambient version history** — SharePoint-style automatic save-boundary snapshots, browsable and restorable, a working tool: nothing review-grade binds to it and it never grounds (informal and QA review live here, as human marked-up edits and comments on the live head, all anchors advisory). A formal cycle instead requires a **freeze** ("check out"): snapshotting the head into an immutable frozen Version at sandbox scope — structurally the same primitive as promotion with a different target scope, so there is **one Version substrate** and everything review-grade (rounds, DRR, anchored comments, renditions) binds to immutable Versions only. While a sandbox-scope round is open the head is locked (ADR-0014's edit lock generalised; audited early release by the lead); the project cycle keeps ADR-0002's live head. The internal cycle is **optional ceremony, never a system gate**: promotion's two legal sources are the live head snapshot or an accepted frozen version — never a drifted head that would silently bypass QA (refining ADR-0002's "snapshots the sandbox head"). The freeze series and the plan container's issued series are two reference sequences (the P-rev/C-rev convention), so the reviewing side's history opens cleanly at v1 with no gaps.

The **cycle is the instance unit**: one launch of the workflow on a container, carrying the appointed sides (inherited from the plan at launch, amendable per ADR-0017), the edit lock, and a state with two terminals — **accepted** and **closed-rejected**. The DRR is not a separate object; it is the cycle's live, append-only record (preserving ADR-0009's per-deliverable reading at project scope: one project cycle per container spanning every issued version). Beneath the cycle: **Rounds** (each on one frozen version, at most one open per cycle — enforced in the schema), **Transmittals** (the flip event in both directions, freezing internal **Snapshots**, ADR-0009/0010 unchanged), **Comments** (under their round), and **Responses** — a first-class record of the mandatory link-or-no-change-with-reason reply (ADR-0004), which is also the comment-to-resolution moat data.

**Rejection pathway:** a rejecting deliverable code leaves resubmission open. The stop is an explicit, audited close to **closed-rejected**, by the lead author (declining to resubmit) or the project owner (so an abandoned deliverable cannot sit "in review" forever). The container state gains **rejected** (ADR-0013): the document stays in the library, badged, its DRR permanently attached — and it never grounds, by construction (retrieval requires `state = live`, and no version was ever accepted). The work item closes on either terminal, mirroring ADR-0003's acceptance close.

**Addendum (2026-07-18, same session — the work-item clarity ruling):** a `document_production` work item is the deliverable's *work half*, linked one-to-one to its Container (the record half; the plan row is their join); the link is nullable by type for future non-document work. Its **status is event-driven, never hand-set** — planned (container created) → drafting (a linked sandbox document exists) → in-review (first project-cycle transmittal) → accepted / rejected (the cycle's terminal) — so "who moves status" is answered: the cycle's events do, refining ADR-0028 §2's runs-drive-status reading. Owner-added reference material is a container with no work item; surfaces must never assume the link exists.

**Consequences:** ADRs 0005–0014's consultant/client language reads as authoring-side/reviewing-side per cycle instance. Drawer 5 is rebuilt around cycle/round/transmittal/snapshot/comment/response; Drawer 2 gains ambient sandbox versioning, the freeze container (Container gains a scope; a sandbox-scope container has no coded reference and no plan row), and the previously missing sandbox-document → planned-container link. The owner's MIDP view shows "in production" until first issue — internal churn is invisible across the wall, consistent with the liability model (T-104): internal QA discipline is the producing firm's own, evidenced in its own record, never enforced or surfaced by Infora. **Parked deliberately:** whether the sandbox-scope cycle reviews on a PDF rendition (ADR-0004) or directly on the frozen native text — establish the workflow, finalise the method, then decide; the comment anchor stays rendition-agnostic either way.
