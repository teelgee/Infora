Story 01

The consultant

Wants to draft the chapter, put it through review, and resolve comments, without ever keeping a separate spreadsheet or chasing a document controller.

1

Planned, not invented

The chapter already exists as a row in the deliverable plan: its reference generated from the project's naming rules, its reviewers named, status not-yet-produced.

Why

Nothing to name or assign in the moment. Promotion later carries almost no metadata burden because it was all set at planning.

ADR-0008
2

Draft in the sandbox

Writes in Word. The drafting assistant grounds only on the project library, never the messy sandbox, so its help is anchored to the project's real positions.

Why

Unfinished drafts never leak into anyone's answers. Grounding comes from a curation decision, not from where a file sits.

ADR-0011
3

Promote v1 ⇄ opens review

Drops the finished draft into its waiting container and promotes. Infora freezes an immutable v1, generates the review PDF, and keeps the native Word file as the owned source.

Why

Versions are immutable snapshots, one in flight at a time. The PDF is the reviewed rendition; the native file stays the asset the client will own.

ADR-0002ADR-0004ADR-0008
4

Blind during review

Sees nothing of the open review while it runs. The comment set only arrives when the lead reviewer closes and issues it.

Why

DRR access is phased: the consultant is downstream of close, never a live feed of comments forming.

ADR-0007
5

Work the returned comments

On close, several of the consultant team open the live DRR table: every comment pinned to the exact place in the v1 PDF it refers to, with its code.

Why

Anchors live on the comment record, bound to the frozen version, so hovering shows the highlighted region. No stale location descriptions.

6

Respond, then v2 ⇄ re-review

Writes a response per comment and cannot mark one addressed without linking to the specific change in the v2 PDF, or stating explicitly that no change was made. Reconciles by hand, then promotes v2.

Why

Mandatory response links yield comment-to-resolution pairs as moat data. Conscious reconciliation, never a silent merge.

7

Accepted

Repeats until the lead reviewer applies an accepting code. The work item closes, the accepted version becomes the controlled record, and it enters the project library automatically.

Why

Acceptance is the trust decision: it both closes the work and admits the content to the knowledge the AI grounds on.

Story 02

The client lead reviewer

Wants reviewers to mark up where they read, to moderate them into one position, and to decide when the chapter is good enough to accept, while keeping the contractual call.

1

Appointed in the plan

Named lead reviewer on the chapter when the deliverable plan was built. Notified when the consultant promotes v1.

Why

Reviewers are appointed at planning, not at transmittal. The transmittal itself is an event record, no file moves on-platform.

ADR-0008ADR-0010
2

Open the v1 PDF

Reviewers, who may sit in different organisations on the project, highlight text or drop a point. Every comment lands in one shared DRR the whole review group sees as it is written.

Why

Cross-organisation openness is contained inside the appointed review group. Comments are pinned to the page, not typed as "page 3, para 4".

ADR-0004ADR-0007
3

Code each comment

Each comment is coded on the project's own classification scheme, where a blocking code must be addressed before the deliverable can be accepted.

Why

The coding scheme is configurable per project; whether a code blocks acceptance is a property of that code in the project's scheme.

ADR-0003
4

Moderate in the open

Edits and amends reviewers' comments and codes, resolves duplicates, and shapes the group's single position before anything goes back.

Why

Moderation happens on the shared surface, not behind a curtain. The lead reviewer holds authority over the review.

ADR-0003ADR-0007
5

Close the round ⇄ issues to consultant

Decides when review is complete; the review period only chases people and flags overdue, it never closes the round. Sets the authoritative deliverable code, then issues the DRR.

Why

Rounds close by a human, never the clock. The system suggests the deliverable code; the lead reviewer's is authoritative. Issuing freezes an internal snapshot.

ADR-0005ADR-0009ADR-0010
6

Read responses on v2

Each consultant response sits beside its comment in the same record. Clicking a response jumps to the highlighted change in the v2 PDF. Approves responses, keeps others open, raises new comments.

Why

One live DRR spanning every version, never a fresh spreadsheet per round. No hunting through v2 for what changed.

ADR-0006ADR-0004
7

Accept

When the chapter meets the standard being paid for, applies an accepting code. The deliverable is accepted, the work item closes, and the accepted version passes into the project library.

Why

Client acceptance against a version is the contractual finish, and the same act that grows the trusted project knowledge.
