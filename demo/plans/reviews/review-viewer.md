# Viewer review of the four walkthrough plans

Written 2026-10-05. Reviewed from the seats of the three people these videos are
for: (a) a document controller or information manager at a large UK
infrastructure consultancy, who works to ISO 19650 every day in ProjectWise,
Aconex or SharePoint; (b) a technical director deciding whether to trust an AI
tool; and (c) a programme lead on the client side at a utility. All three have
seen the Nomic, Build and Harvey demos.

**In plain words.** The four plans are a big step up from the first set. They
show real work in a real-looking product, with one project, one cast and
consistent data. The problems that are left are not about style. They are small
details a professional would notice, and they would undo the realism. In W1,
the consultant's answer to a comment points at the wrong passage. In W2, one
answer does not actually answer its question. In W3, the "discussion" asks
about something the paragraph already cites. In W4, a mistake the QA checker
catches is also in the document that W1 had just accepted. There are also a few
pacing issues. The beats that carry each video's main point are the shortest
ones on screen: the wall in W1, the access comparison in W2, and the totals
changing in W4. Each video has its five changes listed below, most important
first. Most of them cost no extra time, because the time can come from cutting
typing or admin steps.

Frame numbers are the plans' video-local frames at 30 fps. Proposed captions
follow the house rules (eight words or fewer, hyphens, no full stop).

## W1 Document management

**Verdict: the most credible of the four. A document controller will recognise
it as their job.** The order is right: a slot in the delivery plan, binding, an
in-house check, a gated promotion, a named lead reviewer, comments pinned to the
words, the wall, S0, a response to every comment, P02, A1, then C01. That is the
ISO 19650 shared-to-published route, shown on the screen and not on a slide,
which Nomic never does. Three things weaken it. The two moments that make the
point (the wall, and the record of comments and answers) are among the shortest
holds in the video. A few data details would make a document controller frown.
And the captions avoid the codes that would earn this audience's trust.

### Top 5 changes, ranked

1. **Give the review record (the "aha") time, and add a Detail shot.** Shot 6.2
   (2949–3149) holds the record for 6.7 s, and two pans take part of that. It
   is the one screen where every comment sits beside its code and its answer.
   This is the claim "authoritative, with a full audit", and a document
   controller will want to read row 1.3's "No change" and its reason.
   - Extend 6.2 to about 9.5 s (2949–3233).
   - After the second pan, add a **Detail m 1.8** for 90 f on the RESPONSE
     column, rows 1.1–1.3, with `Changed in P02` / `Changed in P02` /
     `No change` stacked.
   - Change caption 14 from "Later, every comment sits beside its answer" to
     **"The record pairs every comment with its answer"**.
   - **Funding:** cut shot 6.7, the library hover (3518–3596, 79 f), and the D3
     crossfade (12 f). End chapter 6 on the accepted status page instead, which
     already shows `C01 — issued on acceptance` and `State ● Live`. W2 opens on
     the library with C01 in it, so the library beat is not lost from the
     series. Cutting 6.7 also removes the only cursor hover next to the `● S2`
     pill (master ruling 1).

2. **Hold the ethical-wall split for twice as long.** Shot 4.8 (1896–1990)
   holds it for 3.2 s. In that time the viewer has to find three highlights
   and three cards on the left, then find their absence and read a 30-word
   amber notice on the right. Nobody will manage that in 3 s, and for the
   client-side viewer this is the reason to buy.
   - Hold the split to 2080 (185 f, 6.2 s) before the 4.9 crossfade.
   - **Funding:** shorten Rachel's typed comment 1 (shot 4.5, 113 characters,
     7.9 s) to **`State made-ground thickness per parcel - the perched water table
     makes it material.`** (83 characters, saves about 60 f). People type a
     hyphen, not an em dash, so this also removes a typed em dash that looks
     scripted. The rest comes from change 4.

3. **Fix two data details a document controller will catch.**
   - **Response 1 points at the wrong passage.** Eleanor types "Made-ground
     thickness now stated per parcel in the CSM table…", then pastes the passage
     `the tiered approach in LCRM Stage 1 before any intrusive scoping`. That is
     a different change, in §4. A reviewer checks exactly this link. Paste a
     passage that matches, for example **`made-ground thickness is tabulated per
     parcel in the CSM table`**. Add that sentence to the P02 PDF's §3, and use
     it in record row 1.1. The built seed has the same mismatch (`24a`), so
     raise it with the owner as well.
   - **Owen's in-house comment is never dealt with.** His S0 comment says
     "Cite the GIR by its accepted revision", and the panel reads `0 of 1
     comment answered` when he passes it. The P01 PDF that Rachel then reviews
     still cites `(WA-GEO-GIR-004)` with no revision. Make P01's §2 read
     **`(WA-GEO-GIR-004 v2)`** in every later version. That costs no time, and
     it shows that the in-house check changed something.

4. **Don't show Owen choosing "A1 — Published — authorized and accepted" for
   an in-house check.** In shot 2.3 (752–926) the whole ISO code list opens at
   Work m 1.4, and Owen picks A1, the client's acceptance code, inside his own
   firm. A document controller will stop at that point: A1 belongs to the
   appointing party, and an internal check is not a publication.
   - End 2.3 on his comment card at Work, without opening the menu (drop
     866–924, about 60 f).
   - Let the promotion window carry the sign-off. It already reads `Source
     version P01 — accepted by internal QA` / `Accepted by Owen Vance, Calder &
     Hale`.
   - Change caption 3 to **"Owen checks it in-house first"**, and add a cue at
     the promotion window: **"Only the version Owen accepted can go"**.
   - Flag the code itself to the owner as a product question: should the
     internal round have its own sign-off code?

5. **Let the captions use the codes. That is where this audience's trust
   comes from.** Master §0 bans the word "suitability" but not the codes, and
   P01, S0, A1 and C01 are the words a document controller is listening for.
   The replacements:
   - Cue 4: "Eleanor sends the checked version to the client" → **"Eleanor issues
     P01 to the client for review"**
   - Cue 6: "Rachel accepts the job and opens the review" → **"Rachel accepts her
     appointment as lead reviewer"**. "The job" sounds casual, and the
     appointment is a contractual act.
   - Cue 9: "That afternoon, Rachel sends the comments back" → **"Rachel returns
     it at S0 for revision"**
   - Cue 15: "Rachel accepts it with the code A1" → **"A1 - authorised and
     accepted"**
   - Cue 16: "Accepted, issued as C01, used for AI answers" → **"C01 issued - the
     contractual revision"**. Keep "the AI may now rely on it" in the narration.
     In this video the point is control, not AI.
   - Also hold shot 4.2's appointment card (1269–1305) for about 45 f more at
     Work m 1.3 on its four bullets. `Only you close a round. No count of
     comments closes it.` is the sentence a document controller would quote back
     to colleagues, and at the moment it goes by in 1.2 s.

**Narration.** Accurate and about the right length (261 words). Two changes
would add credibility for viewer (a). At 250, say "Calder & Hale's delivery
plan - its TIDP - already holds a slot". At 2105, say "She closes the round
at S0 - work in progress, back to the authors" instead of "She closes the round
with S0". Without that gloss, an information manager will hear S0 as a status
error.

**Pacing note.** Chapter 3 (Promote, 11 s) and chapter 4 (Review, 31 s) are out
of proportion. With change 2 the split is part of chapter 4, which is fine, but
keep the total at or under 130 s. With changes 1, 2 and 4 the plan nets about
+40 f, which gives 129.2 s.

## W2 Context layer

**Verdict: the strongest proof beat of the series, with one answer that would
cost the technical director's trust.** The Ask answer is excellent. It has six
claims built from source, pathway and receptor, figures that a land quality
engineer would recognise (2.4 m made ground, benzo(a)pyrene 18 mg/kg, perched
water at 1.6–2.1 m), a source pill on every claim, and a footer that says the
claims were checked by code, not by a second model. Clicking the pill and
landing on the highlighted passage on page 14 is the beat no competitor
finishes on screen. The weak points are these:
- The access comparison ends on an answer that does not answer its question.
- The ontology chapter spends 19 s on administration and a filter that leads
  nowhere.
- The superseded-revision idea is set up in chapter 1 and never paid off.

### Top 5 changes, ranked

1. **Change Q3 so that Eleanor's answer is a real answer.** Q3 is "What
   protected species survey work has been done along the corridor?" Eleanor's
   answer 3E is two claims about arable land and brown trout. That is not
   survey work. A technical director will read it as the AI dodging the
   question, which is worse than saying nothing, and it suggests that limited
   access gives misleading answers. Ask a question both seats can answer
   honestly: **`What ecological receptors are recorded along Ashcombe Brook?`**
   (61 characters, about 20 f quicker to type than now).
   - Rachel's answer (3R) keeps the appraisal's habitat, newt and badger
     claims (`WA-ECO-PEA-001`) and the baseline's trout claim.
   - Eleanor's answer (3E) keeps its two `WA-ENV-BAS-003` claims (the arable
     land crossed by the brook, and brown trout with WFD Moderate). Both are now
     on topic.
   - The lesson stays the same, and is more honest: Eleanor's answer is true,
     just narrower.

2. **Give the access comparison time to be compared.** Both answers stream at
   once (3022–3157), and then the final hold is 3.6 s (3158–3266). The viewer
   has to see that the left has `WA-ECO-PEA-001` pills and the right has none,
   and nothing points them to it.
   - Extend 6.6 by 90 f.
   - Add a cue between 16 and 17 at about 3165: **"Eleanor's answer has no
     Redgate source, no hint"** (7 words).
   - Keep the required line (cue 17) after it, held for 80 f.
   - **Funding:** cut shot 2.7, the "Land quality" facet filter (1052–1196,
     145 f), and caption 7 with it. The filter is a vision control that leads
     nowhere in the story. Chapter 2 drops to about 14 s, which is in better
     proportion for a beat that is all vision.

3. **Pay off the superseded revision on screen.** Chapter 1 shows `v1 ·
   Superseded … not used for AI answers` but never shows why that matters. The
   seed has the perfect trap: v1 said "Groundwater was not encountered" and v2
   says perched water at 1.6–2.1 m. Claim 4 of answer 1 depends on v2, and the
   source panel shows `v2 ● Live`. Change caption 14 (2542) from "The cited
   passage, on the live revision" to **"From the live v2 - v1 found no
   groundwater"** (8 words). Add one line of narration at 2692 to replace
   "Every citation opens in one click": **"Version one found no groundwater. The
   answer never saw it."** For a document controller, this is the moment the
   AI respects revision control.

4. **Make chapter 3's link to the ontology readable, and say less in the
   chips.** Shot 3.4 (1390–1470) holds the related group for 2.7 s, and one
   reason chip reads `Derived-from source of WA-EIA-MST-001`, which is graph
   shorthand.
   - Change that chip to **`A source of the accepted method statement`**.
   - Hold Work m 1.4 for about 30 f more before the first tick.
   - Change caption 9 from "Related documents come first, with the reason"
     to **"Related documents come first, each with a reason"**. The current
     line reads as if one reason covers them all.
   - The deck line "every new document starts from it" needs this beat to
     land, and chapter 2's links are exactly what puts these documents on top.
     Narration at 1396 could say so: "The links Rachel just confirmed are why
     these come first."

5. **Shorten Q1, and give the finished answer a reading hold.** Q1 types for
   8.2 s (1745–1990, 118 characters), and the complete answer then holds for
   only 51 f before chapter 5.
   - Type **`What did the GI find at Wrenfield Sidings, and what does it mean
     for dewatering near the brook?`** (95 characters, saving about 45 f).
     Engineers say "GI", and it reads as a real person typing.
   - Move the saved frames into 4.6, so the answer and footer (including the
     honesty line, which the technical director will read) hold for about
     3.2 s.
   - Change caption 5 from "The accepted statement arrives with three
     proposed links" to **"Three links proposed - none live until
     confirmed"**. The silent viewer otherwise doesn't know who proposed them,
     or that confirming is the control.

**Narration.** 240 words. That is under the band, but correct for the
silences. "The project ontology shows how the library connects" is the only term
of art left. Precede it with "Infora classifies every document in the
library…", so that a programme lead does not need the word.

## W3 Collaboration

**Verdict: the right device (two windows, two cursors, one event landing on
both at once), but the "together" is thinner than it looks, and the content of
the collaboration would not survive a professional reading it.** At present
only Nadia writes in the document. Eleanor writes in her own chat panel. So the
co-editing beat is one person typing while the other watches, and the other
person's chat can't be seen by the first. The AI's dewatering paragraph then
lands under Nadia's heading "1. Ecological receptors". Nadia's question asks
Eleanor to confirm the flood-zone fact the paragraph already cites from the
FRA, and Eleanor's reply repeats it. A technical director would see a staged
exchange. The fixes keep the structure and change who types what.

### Top 5 changes, ranked

1. **Make chapter 3 real co-editing: both of them write in the canvas.** In
   3.3 (1394–1629), Nadia types §1 as now. In the same seconds, **Eleanor
   types a heading lower in the same document: `2. Dewatering and the
   brook`** (27 characters), instead of her chat prompt. Each canvas then shows
   the other person's flagged caret, so there are two flags, two carets and
   two texts growing in both windows. That is the "true multiplayer" the brief
   asks for, and it is the thing Nomic, Build and Harvey never show. Two
   problems are fixed with it:
   - The AI paragraph now lands under a heading that fits it. The built accept
     appends to the end, which is under Eleanor's §2.
   - Change caption 6 to **"Both write in the same document at once"**.

2. **Move Eleanor's prompt into chapter 4, and make it shorter.** With change
   1, she types the request at the start of chapter 4. Shorten it to **`Draft
   this section on how dewatering could affect the brook, per`** (63
   characters, about 130 f), then insert the two library references as now.
   - Chapter 4 needs about +4 s. Take it from the 7.7 s static hold in 4.5
     (2136–2366). Shorten that hold to about 4 s and start Nadia's hover in
     chapter 5 sooner.
   - Caption 7 stays as it is.

3. **Rewrite the discussion so the question is one only a person can
   answer.** At present Nadia asks "@Eleanor Price can we confirm pit locations
   against the FRA?" about a sentence already cited to `WA-WAT-FRA-001`, and
   Eleanor's reply restates it. Make it a real ecology-meets-land-quality
   question on the word **"dewatering"** in the accepted paragraph:
   - Nadia: **`@Eleanor Price will dewatering discharge to the brook? It holds
     brown trout.`**
   - Eleanor: **`No discharge to the brook - the dewatering management plan will
     set that out.`**
   - This rests on the GIR's own recommendation of "a dewatering management
     plan", so it is a human commitment and contradicts nothing in the record.
     Check it against the seed and the CoCP wording before building.
   - The typed length is about the same as now, so the timing barely changes.
   - Change caption 12 to **"Nadia raises the trout with Eleanor"**.

4. **Nadia, the ecologist, should check the ecology claim.** In shot 5.1 she
   hovers the `WA-GEO-GIR-004` pill, and the card shows the perched-water
   passage. That is the same page-14 passage W2 holds in Detail, and W4 shows a
   GIR hover too. An ecologist would check the trout sentence.
   - Hover **`WA-ENV-BAS-003`** after claim 3. The card shows `WA-ENV-BAS-003 ·
     v3 · ● Live`, and the highlighted sentence is "The Ashcombe Brook supports
     brown trout and is classified Moderate under the Water Framework Directive
     (Table 9)."
   - Change caption 10 to **"Nadia checks the ecology source, then
     accepts"**.
   - Narration at 2375: "Before trusting it, Nadia checks the ecology
     claim against the baseline."

5. **Trim the setup and lengthen Owen's beat.** Chapter 1 (24 s) and chapter 2
   (13 s) are 31% of the video, spent on administration. Meanwhile, Owen's
   proof that he can't see the space (1.10, 863–926) lasts 2.1 s, with no
   cursor and nothing to compare against.
   - Type the sandbox name as **`Ashcombe Brook crossing`** (23 characters,
     saving about 45 f). The full name with a typed em dash looks scripted.
   - Shorten the document title in 2.3 to **`Land quality and ecology
     interface`** (34 characters, saving about 50 f). It types at Wide at
     14.8 px, which nobody can read anyway.
   - Give Owen 120 f (4 s), with his cursor resting on the two cards.
   - Narration at 212: "a working area outside the document controls" will
     alarm a document controller ("uncontrolled!"). Say **"a shared
     work-in-progress area - nothing in it is issued until it is promoted
     through review"**.

**Note for the owner.** After acceptance, the paragraph is plain prose and the
source pills are gone. That is accurate, because the built canvas is plain
text, but the close frame then shows a document with no citations. The
technical director will notice. Don't let the narration imply that the
citations persist. The close caption "Two firms, one document, nothing emailed"
is good.

## W4 Tools

**Verdict: the app builder is the most impressive thing in the series, and the
chat-led build, with three questions it will not skip, answers the "AI
cowboy" fear well. But the video is overstuffed, it jumps backwards in time
without saying so, and its two payoffs (the preview filling in, and the totals
moving) are too small or too fast to see.** The KPI data is very good. A
utility programme lead will recognise the content straight away: NCRs, on-time
deliveries, carbon, local spend, a 90% flag, "Ashcombe Civils accounted for 6
of 9 late deliveries", and returns that count once accepted. The figures add
up. The builder chapter is 43 s, a third of the runtime, and needs the camera's
help.

### Top 5 changes, ranked

1. **Say the time jump on screen, both ways.** The video runs Oct 05 (QA
   check, creator), then jumps silently back to June (Rachel's Tools page says
   `No apps yet`), then "Months later". A silent viewer won't follow it. The
   narration says "Earlier in the project", but the captions don't.
   - Caption 9 (1473): "Rachel describes the app in her own words" → **"In
     June, Rachel describes the app she needs"** (8 words).
   - Caption 16 (2748): "Months later, the returns are in" → **"October -
     Netherfold submits its September return"** (6 words).

2. **Let the viewer see the preview fill in. It is the builder's "aha".** The
   preview is drawn at a 0.525 scale inside a 364 px card, at Wide. Its text is
   about 8 px on screen, so the moment the app appears (2213–2240) and the
   flag line arriving (2491–2502) can't be read. Q2 also streams across the
   preview's fill, and the camera rule forbids moving during streaming.
   - Delay Q2 by about 60 f.
   - Insert a Work m 1.6 push on the preview at about (1326, 460): move

   - Do the same around the flag line: move 2503–2523, hold 40 f, back to Wide.

3. **Frame the totals changing, and caption the rule.** In 5.4 (3121–3140),
   four tiles, a bar, a line point and the donut centre all change within 20 f,

   the narration but not the captions, and it is the line a client-side

   - During the confirm-strip hold, move to Work m 1.3 at (870, 420)

   - Go straight from `See the plan` to `Covers:` to accept. That saves about
     75 f, and the difference from W2 is then the saved firm outline and the
     coverage numbers.
   - Use part of the saving in chapter 2: hold the accuracy hover card in 2.3

     finding ("Intrusive investigation across the Wrenfield Sidings compound…
     three of eleven trial pits (WS-TP03, WS-TP07, WS-TP09)"). At present it
     holds about 40 words for 2 s.

5. **Don't end on a repeat. Close on the product's claim.** The close caption
   "One app, each firm sees its own" nearly repeats the required line at cue

   - Close on **"Built by asking - used across two organisations"** (7 words).
     It states both halves of the aha.
   - Replace the title's deck line (see the cross-video notes) with a scenario

**Narration.** 269 words, natural, and inside the claims list. "The figures are

## Cross-video notes

1. **A continuity bug that undermines W1.** W4's accuracy finding is "The record
   puts these PAH hotspots at Wrenfield Sidings, not south of the Wrenfield
   substation". But W1's document, `WA-EIA-MST-001`, which went through two
   review rounds and was accepted at A1 as C01, says exactly that in §2: "PAH
   exceedances at three trial pits south of the Wrenfield substation" (W1 §4.6,
   §4.12). Anyone who watches both will conclude that Infora's review process
   let a factual error into the contractual record. Change W1's text in every
   version to **"at three trial pits at Wrenfield Sidings"**, and keep the error
   only in W4's working copy. W2's answer already says Wrenfield Sidings.

2. **Replace the deck line on each title card with a scenario line.** The
   master uses the deck's taglines ("Best-in-class information management,
   seamless by design.") on all four title cards. That is the salesy register
   the owner rejected. The benchmark research also recommends a scenario line
   naming the work. Suggested lines:
   - W1: **"WA-EIA-MST-001, from first draft to C01"**
   - W2: **"One question, answered from the project library"**
   - W3: **"Two firms write one document together"**
   - W4: **"A QA check, a saved workflow, and a supplier KPI app"**
   Keep the deck lines for the narration, if anywhere.

3. **Too many clicks on the same source.** The GIR page-14 passage is opened
   in W2 (in Detail), hovered in W3, and a GIR hover appears again in W4.
   Change W3 to the baseline (W3 change 4), so each video's source check shows
   something different.

4. **The creator appears twice** (W2 chapter 3 and W4 chapter 3, with the same
   person and the same two sources). W2's beat is about why the sources are
   related, and W4's is about the saved outline and the coverage. Keep W2's
   beat and compress W4's (W4 change 4). Also, W2's sandbox (§4.5) already
   lists W4's output `Land contamination — Ashcombe Lane parcels`, and Eleanor
   then starts another land quality document. Give W2's new document a
   different title in the caption, or name it in the narration as a second
   note, so the two don't read as the same thing done twice.

5. **One project, one cast: this works.** Rachel and Eleanor appear in all
   four videos, Owen's two roles fit together (checker in W1, excluded in W3),
   and W2 opens on the C01 that W1 issued. One stretch: Rachel is the
   programme owner, the lead reviewer of a land quality method statement, the
   creator of the sandbox and the builder of the app. A client-side viewer will
   accept that for cast economy, but the narration should never call her
   anything more specific than "for the client".

6. **The order is right.** W1 makes the record, W2 asks it questions, W3 writes
   new work beside it, and W4 extends the product. The in-world dates run
   W1 → W3 (Oct 02) → W2 and W4 (Oct 05), but no caption carries a date, so
   nobody will notice.

7. **Codes and terms.** Across the series, use the real codes (P01, S0, A1,
   C01, v2) in the captions wherever they are on screen. Keep the plain
   meaning beside them ("A1 - authorised and accepted"). That is what tells
   viewer (a) this was made by people who know the job. The internal words
   still on screen, `Promote`, `sandbox`, `playbook` and `bind`, are product
   UI and fine. The captions should translate them, as most already do.
