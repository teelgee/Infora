# ADR-0023 - The authority register (legislation, policy, standards) is owned within Context as the universal authority corpus, Infora-maintained; it is a grounded corpus retrievable by users during generation, not only a compliance-checking input

**Status:** Accepted. **Extended 2026-07-29 (owner-ruled, the Ask agent-harness Phase 2 gate — [docs/build/plan.md — Slice Ask](../build/plan.md)) — the authority corpus is two-tier, not one:** this ADR fixed the register as universal, Infora-maintained and outside per-tenant access resolution. That describes **one tier of two**. The **standard tier** is as described — Infora-shipped and universal: the Planning Act 2008 and the EIA and Habitats Regulations, National Policy Statements, PINS Advice Notes, s.51 advice, precedent DCOs and Decision Letters. The **organisation tier** is new: an organisation's owner adds its own standards, guidance and policy — licensed standards it subscribes to (ISO, BSI), sector guidance, and its own policy instruments — held in that organisation's **portable org context** (ADR-0020) and travelling with it to every project it works on.

Three things follow, each a real change to what this ADR said. **(1) Access.** "Outside per-tenant access resolution (the law is the law)" holds for the standard tier only. The organisation tier is org-private on the ADR-0018 private floor: it never crosses the wall, and a retrieval resolves the standard tier ∪ the requester's own organisation tier — so two organisations on one project legitimately hold different authority shelves and can legitimately get different answers to the same question. That is correct, and it is stated rather than hidden.

**(2) It still grounds and cites, despite living in org context.** This does not breach ADR-0020's "org context informs only", because ADR-0021 already fixed that **surface placement does not set channel role**. The operative distinction is not *where material sits* or *who uploaded it* but *what it is*: a firm's **own** material — precedent, know-how, method — informs and is never cited, because its provenance is the firm's and its confidentiality is entangled with clients' (ADR-0026 Filters 1 and 2). A **published external instrument** with an issuing body, a version and an in-force date grounds and is cited, whoever loaded it, because none of that is true of it. ISO 19650 is ISO 19650 regardless of which shelf resolved it.

**(3) Licensing inverts, and this is the commercially important part.** The standard tier is deliberately confined to material Infora can ship freely — UK legislation, NPSs, PINS advice, DCOs and Decision Letters are Crown copyright under the Open Government Licence — so **Infora does not buy per-customer subscriptions to paid standards.** Where a firm needs ISO or BSI material, the firm holds the licence it already holds and adds it to its own shelf. The licensing gate this ADR names therefore becomes an org-tier concern, and Infora's exposure is the standard tier only.

**Currency splits with the tiers.** Infora operates in-force currency for the standard tier (commencement, amendment, supersession by date). It cannot know that a customer's uploaded standard has been superseded, so an organisation-tier entry carries an **org-declared** in-force interval and a last-reviewed date, and the currency note distinguishes the two — "Infora-maintained" versus "org-declared, last reviewed <date>" — rather than implying one guarantee for both. **Revised 2026-06-27:** re-homed from the Compliance capability to **Context** after the features/components reframe dissolved Compliance as a component — compliance-*checking* became a capability inside the Engine, and the authority register became part of Context, where the grounded corpora live. The substance below (dual-purpose, generation-first-class, universal/public/Infora-maintained, its own lifecycle and licensing gate) is unchanged. Builds on ADR-0021 (the authority corpus as the grounded channel's second, universal corpus) and ADR-0022 (its retrieval as public and licence-gated).

**Context:** The grounded channel's second corpus — legislation, policy and standards (ADR-0021) — needed a home in the component model. It is unlike the three tenant context stores: universal, public, cross-tenant, and maintained by Infora rather than by any customer, with its own lifecycle (in-force currency, amendments, jurisdiction, licensing of paid standards). It is also consumed two ways: a compliance-checking capability tests documents against it, and the grounded channel cites it when a user drafts. The original fork was whether it is a standalone component, a sub-component of Compliance, or a part of Context. The reframe then settled the component model itself: **Compliance is no longer a component** — checking is a capability the Engine runs — so the register is owned where the other grounded corpora already live, in Context. The risk to avoid is the register looking like a checking-only asset, which would hide that it must be retrievable during ordinary document generation.

**Decision:** The authority register is owned within the **Context component**, holding the universal **authority corpus** — the second grounded corpus alongside the project library (ADR-0021). Context operates its lifecycle: ingestion of legislation, policy and standards; in-force currency (commencement, amendment, supersession by date); jurisdiction tagging; and a licensing gate for paid standards (an entitlement check, distinct from the ethical wall).

The register is **dual-purpose, and the generation purpose is first-class**. It is a grounded corpus retrievable by users when generating documentation — drafting and chat ground on and cite legislation and standards directly through the grounded channel (ADR-0021, ADR-0022) — as well as the reference the **compliance-checking capability** (a skill run by the Engine) tests documents against. The checking capability *reads* the corpus from Context; it does not own it, and grounding is never routed through a compliance run.

**Consequences:** The authority corpus sits in Context beside the per-tenant stores but is unlike them — universal, Infora-maintained, public, and outside per-tenant access resolution (the law is the law), gated only by standards licensing. Its encoding format, currency cadence and v1 jurisdiction scope remain open — now a Context / authority-register concern, formerly tracked under the compliance capability (to re-home in the grill tracker). The dual-purpose nature must be explicit in surfaces and APIs: a user drafting can pull and cite a clause without invoking a compliance run, so the register exposes a retrieval path for the grounded channel that does not depend on the checking path. Compliance-checking, as an Engine capability, depends on the corpus but does not own it. (ADR-0024's note that the authority corpus is the one corpus that may later split out of pgvector still holds — that is a store-level split, not a change of component home.)
