# ADR-0014 - DRR visibility is permanent for every issued round; only the open round is hidden from the consultant; one edit lock alternates between reviewers and consultant at each transmittal

**Status:** Accepted. Refines ADR-0007.

**Context:** ADR-0007 phased DRR access "per deliverable" with a single open/issued flip and said the consultant has no live visibility, receiving a moderated comment set only at issue. That framing is too coarse for a deliverable that runs across many rounds. By round three the consultant has already seen and responded to rounds one and two, so the DRR cannot go dark on the consultant each time a new round opens. ADR-0007 also settled only who may see the DRR, never who may write to it at each phase. Separating visibility from write rights resolves both at once.

**Decision:** Split DRR access into visibility and write rights.

Visibility: every party granted access to the deliverable sees the one live DRR (ADR-0009) at all times, across every round, with a single exception. The currently-open round's comments are hidden from the consultant until the lead reviewer issues that round. Every round that has been issued stays permanently visible to everyone, including the consultant. Within the open round, comments remain live and cross-organisation to the appointed review group, exactly as ADR-0007 set.

Write rights: a single edit lock decides who may write to the DRR, and it alternates. While a round is open, only reviewers may write, that is add and code comments, and the lead reviewer moderates and sets the authoritative deliverable code. At transmittal out (issue) the lock flips: the authoring team may write responses and reviewers may not. When the lead author promotes the next version and transmits it back, the lock flips again to the reviewers for the new round. Transmittal is the flip event in both directions; at issue it does two jobs at once, revealing the closed round to the consultant and handing the consultant the pen.

This rests on strict sequentiality: only one round is ever open, because a deliverable carries one in-flight controlled revision at a time (ADR-0002, ADR-0005), and a new round cannot open until the authoring team has responded and the lead author has promoted the next version. So the consultant always sees every round except the single open one. DRR access, both visibility and the edit lock, is owned by the access model (component 1); the DRR and transmittal components consume it.

**Consequences:** The append-only history (ADR-0009) is trivially consistent, because there is one record everyone reads and nothing ever disappears from view. The ethical-wall exception stays narrow and points the right way: cross-organisation reviewer deliberation in the open round is walled from the consultant until the lead has moderated and issued it, so reviewer-side positioning is never exposed live. Integrity now comes from the edit lock, not from hiding the record: the consultant cannot alter comments while reviewers write them, and reviewers cannot rewrite a round once issued. The guarantee that the consultant sees every round but the open one depends entirely on the one-open-round invariant; if two rounds were ever open at once the guarantee would break, so the invariant must hold. This supersedes ADR-0007's "no live visibility" and "two scopes per deliverable" framing and replaces it with per-round permanent visibility plus an alternating edit lock; the rest of ADR-0007 stands. Builds on ADR-0002, ADR-0005, ADR-0007, ADR-0009 and ADR-0010.
