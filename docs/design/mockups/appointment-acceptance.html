Design decisions in this mockup
Two acts, worded as two different things "Accept the appointment" is about you; "Acknowledge the submission" is about the document. They sit in different places on the screen — the undertaking in the body, the receipt in the pane header — so they never read as one confirmation split in two.
The undertaking states the duty, not the mechanism Four lines on what holding this role means: you set the code, only you close a round, you hold the accepting code, you manage the reviewers. Nothing about tables or grants. A professional undertaking should be readable by the person giving it.
The gate explains itself where it bites In State A the acknowledge button is disabled and the Submission card says why, including the part people will otherwise get wrong: a super user cannot acknowledge in your place while the appointment is unaccepted. Their move is to name someone else.
Accepting is not tinted The undertaking card is the plain sunken card. It is a decision asked of you, not a warning about a failure, and hue stays reserved for status — which is why the badge on your own row carries the amber, and the card does not.
A decline is a state, not an error State C says what happened, who did it, and what happens next, and ends on "nothing is lost and nothing has failed". The reason stays inside the owner organisation; the producing side learns only that a lead reviewer is awaited.
Ruled — no notification, and the cost of that Notifications are deferred (owner, 2026-08-22): Slice V1-N is unbuilt and an appointment event was never in ADR-0049's pilot set of two, so the rail is not drawn here. The consequence is stated rather than designed around — an appointed lead reviewer is told nothing, and finds out by opening the deliverable or by the consultant chasing them. The gate holds either way; the submission simply waits longer.
